# Answer key — Northline Home — network redesign

> EVALUATOR ONLY. Never include this file in a generation workspace.

## Expected initial-hypothesis handling

**keep_with_conditions**: The proposed central consolidation yields little net annual saving, violates service in modeled conditions and exhausts capacity; keeping East is reasonable subject to a measurable optimization and future cross-dock gate.

## Key insights

- **K1** **critical**: East fixed cost saved is $1.30M but higher Eastern freight adds $1.00M and handling $0.20M; net annual saving only $0.10M before exit fee.
- **K2** **critical**: Central load after full East closure would be 360,000, exactly its practical capacity, and 2028 forecast rises to 389,600.
- **K3** **critical**: Modeled Eastern two-day rate falls 96% to 84%, but the contract tests company-wide 92%; aggregate service must be calculated on comparable all-ZIP orders.
- **K4** **critical**: 98% internal East on-time claim excludes rural ZIPs and must not replace 96% contracted measure.
- **K5**: Cross-dock had 93% performance over a limited six-week pilot, not a contract guarantee for peak season.
- **K6**: Keep and optimize saves $0.30M annually for $0.15M systems spend, with a 2029 lease option preserved.

## Required calculations

- **Q1**: Full close incremental transport and handling — expected **$1.00M + $0.20M = $1.20M**, tolerance ±$0.02M
- **Q2**: Annual net saving of closing East — expected **$0.10M before exit fee**, tolerance ±$0.02M
- **Q3**: Central capacity in 2028 with East — expected **389,600 vs 360,000 practical**, tolerance ±500 shipments
- **Q4**: 2027 company-wide SLA under closure — expected **200k×84%+160k×94%+140k×95% over 500k = 90.3%**, tolerance ±0.2 pp

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **E1** **critical**: Two-day all-order service floor and Central capacity cap
- **E2** **critical**: Fixed plus freight/handling cost and one-time exit charge
- **E3** **critical**: All-ZIP definition and rural exclusions
- **E4**: Cross-dock peak-season evidence quality

## Reasoning traps

- **T1**: Counting $1.30M gross facility savings as net saving
- **T2**: Treating Eastern SLA as network-wide without weighting
- **T3**: Ignoring Central capacity once Eastern and Central volume grow
- **T4**: Treating six-week cross-dock pilot as peak-season proof

## Key tradeoffs

- **X1**: Fixed cost vs delivery time
- **X2**: Consolidation now vs reversible lease option
- **X3**: Third-party dependence vs operating capacity

## Plausible recommendations

- **R1**: Keep East in 2027, take verified $0.30M efficiency programme, and test a properly specified peak-season cross-dock contract. [defensible: yes]
- **R2**: Move selected East lanes to a partner if contractual all-ZIP SLA, capacity and net cost are proven; preserve the 2029 lease option. [defensible: conditional]
- **R3**: Close East and shift all orders to Central without extra capacity or SLA mitigation. [defensible: no]

## Decision requirements

- **D1** **critical**: Does the recommendation satisfy company-wide service SLA? (acceptable evidence examples: weighted delivery-service calculation, pilot comparison)
- **D2** **critical**: Does Central stay within certified capacity under growth? (acceptable evidence examples: network volume model, capacity scenario)
- **D3** **critical**: Is net cost based on comparable definitions? (acceptable evidence examples: fully loaded cost comparison, exit cost)
- **D4**: Is the outsourced option assessed fairly? (acceptable evidence examples: crossdock pilot and contract trigger)

## Contradictory evidence


- 98% selective-ZIP internal KPI vs 96% contract-compliant East rate
- Retention premium correlated with Eastern sales coverage
- Fixed cost removed vs added transport and damage

## Defensibility criteria

- Model all-in cost not warehouse rent only
- Weight SLA using all shipments and same ZIP definition
- Recognize the 2028 Central capacity breach
- Quantify a realistic alternative and decision gate
