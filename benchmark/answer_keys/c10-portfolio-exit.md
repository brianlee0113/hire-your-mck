## Expected initial-hypothesis handling

**reject**: C has high direct contribution despite allocated overhead; an immediate exit loses cash contribution and triggers contractual penalties. Redesign or an orderly future sunset is more defensible.

## Key insights

- **K1** **critical**: C generates $3.8M direct contribution on $6M sales, despite an 8.3% reported margin after $3.3M shared allocation.
- **K2** **critical**: Immediate C exit with the maximum D transfer changes annual operating contribution by $-2.65M before contract penalties; first year $-4.75M.
- **K3** **critical**: Only $0.4M of C allocated overhead is avoidable; shared regulatory and depreciation costs do not simply disappear.
- **K4** **critical**: Nine customer bundles require C, with $2.1M first-year penalties and 95% bundle service requirement.
- **K5**: Redesign could save $1.25M annually for $0.55M one time, subject to customer validation and implementation.
- **K6**: Renewal gap is not causal evidence because C accounts are older, smaller and less-supported.

- **Q1**: C direct contribution — expected **$6.0M − $2.2M = $3.8M**, tolerance ±$0.05M
- **Q2**: Exit annual net impact (optimistic D transfer) — expected **$0.4M + $1.5M×50% − $3.8M = $-2.65M**, tolerance ±$0.05M
- **Q3**: First-year impact including contract penalty — expected **$-4.75M**, tolerance ±$0.05M

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **E1** **critical**: Direct-variable costs versus allocated shared costs
- **E2** **critical**: Nine bundle contracts and $2.1M exit penalty
- **E3** **critical**: D substitution limited to $1.5M incremental revenue
- **E4**: Bundle-level service definition and patient device validation

## Reasoning traps

- **T1**: Equating a low allocated margin with negative avoidable contribution
- **T2**: Assuming all shared overhead is saved on exit
- **T3**: Treating C ownership as a cause of lower renewal
- **T4**: Ignoring bundle penalty and validation

## Key tradeoffs

- **X1**: Portfolio simplicity vs contribution cash
- **X2**: D growth vs customer-service continuity
- **X3**: Immediate exit vs validated redesign

## Plausible recommendations

- **R1**: Retain C for now, fix bundle service and test the $0.55M redesign, then reconsider sunset when contracts renew. [defensible: yes]
- **R2**: Pursue a negotiated phased exit if customer consents and new capacity/transfer economics establish positive value. [defensible: conditional]
- **R3**: Discontinue C immediately based only on the 8% reported margin. [defensible: no]

## Decision requirements

- **D1** **critical**: Are product costs classified by avoidability? (acceptable evidence examples: contribution bridge, allocation reconciliation)
- **D2** **critical**: Does exit respect bundle contracts? (acceptable evidence examples: contract penalties, service obligations)
- **D3** **critical**: Is D substitution backed by realistic capacity and qualification? (acceptable evidence examples: incremental transfer scenario, capacity check)
- **D4** **critical**: Is the comparison more than reported gross margin? (acceptable evidence examples: exit versus redesign economics, decision gate)

## Contradictory evidence


- C gross margin is allocated operating metric, not incremental contribution
- C customers renew less but differ in size and support
- Item-level service dashboard hides whole-bundle delays

## Defensibility criteria

- Reconcile margins to avoidable cash flows
- Apply contract penalty separately from annual cost
- Bound D substitution with capacity and validation
- Avoid causal inference from account-mix correlation
