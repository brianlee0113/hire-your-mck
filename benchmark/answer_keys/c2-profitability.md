# Answer key · Harlow Fresh Markets — reversing the margin decline

> **Evaluators only.** Never copy this file into a generation workspace.

**Decision question:** How should Harlow reverse its declining operating margin, and which actions should it take first?

## Initial hypothesis in the pack

- Holder: CFO and COO
- Statement: Wage inflation is the main cause; cut store labor hours 8% and freeze e-commerce.
- Expected handling: **revise**
- Why: Store labor explains only about $4.5M of the $32.6M decline in operating income (FY2023→FY2025). The largest drivers are e-commerce fulfillment ($12.6M), promotions ($10.5M) and shrink ($7.5M). Wage rates rose but hours per $1,000 of sales fell, and low-labor stores show the highest shrink, so an 8% hours cut risks raising shrink. Freezing e-commerce leaves a loss-making service as it is. A good answer revises the diagnosis.

## Key analytical insights

- **K1** (critical): Operating income fell from $47.9M to $15.3M (margin 3.37% → 1.02%).
- **K2** (critical): Bridge FY2023→FY2025 (at FY2025 sales): sales growth +$2.8M; e-commerce fulfillment $-12.6M; promotions $-10.5M; shrink $-7.5M; store labor $-4.5M; occupancy/energy $-3.0M; private label +$2.7M. Labor is about 14% of the decline.
- **K3** (critical): E-commerce loses money: net fulfillment cost $18.1M in FY2025 ($13.61 per order) against only about $7.7M of contribution on the 30% of e-commerce sales that are incremental: a net drag of about $10.4M a year.
- **K4** (critical): The fee test shows a lever: higher fee and threshold cut delivered orders 9% but raised fee per delivered order from $3.10 to $6.40 and shifted orders to click-and-collect, at a 2% loss of e-commerce sales (mostly cannibalised anyway).
- **K5** (critical): Labor cuts conflict with shrink: lowest-labor quartile has shrink of 3.1% vs 2.3% in the highest; an 8% hours cut (~$14.5M) could be partly offset by higher shrink (each 0.1 pp of shrink is about $1.5M).
- **K6**: Promotional growth is partly a competitive response to ValuMart (−4.2% comps near new stores), and vendor funding fell; 38% of events lose money — cut the losing events rather than all promotions.
- **K7**: Closing the nine weakest stores at lease expiry improves operating income by about $6.6M a year when all nine are closed (removing −$3.1M EBITDA plus 25% × $118M × 12% transferred), but three need a $7.5M exit cost and a board vote.
- **K8**: Target: 3.0% of ~$1.5B sales needs roughly $45M operating income, about $30M more than FY2025; the plan must add up to that within $25M one-time cost and protect the $20M FY2026 covenant.
- **K9**: The e-commerce team's 6.1% (≈$7.8M) leaves out third-party delivery (≈$8.3M net of fees) and cannibalisation; with delivery alone it is ≈$-0.5M, and counting only incremental sales the service is a ≈$10M drag.

## Required calculations

- **Q1**: Operating margin by year — expected: 3.37% → 2.27% → 1.02% (tolerance: ±0.05 pp)
- **Q2**: Margin/operating-income bridge by driver — expected: sales_growth_at_2023_margin: +2.8; shrink: -7.5; promotions: -10.5; private_label: +2.7; store_labor: -4.5; occupancy_energy: -3.0; ecommerce_fulfillment: -12.6; total: -32.6 (tolerance: each driver ±$1M; ranking must be right (e-com, promo, shrink > labor))
- **Q3**: E-commerce net cost per order and economics — expected: $13.61 per order; net drag ≈ $10.4M (tolerance: ±$0.5 per order; drag $8–12M)
- **Q4**: Size of the 3.0% target gap — expected: ≈ $29.8M more operating income at FY2025 sales (tolerance: ±$3M)
- **Q5**: Store closure impact — expected: $6.6M (tolerance: ±$1M)

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **E1** (critical): Income statement lines showing which costs grew as a share of sales
- **E2** (critical): E-commerce per-order costs, fees and 70% cannibalisation
- **E3** (critical): Shrink by labor-hours quartile
- **E4** (critical): Promotional share, 14 pp margin gap, vendor funding fall, 38% losing events
- **E5**: Fee test results
- **E6**: ValuMart competitive pressure and price perception
- **E7**: Weak-store data and lease expiries
- **E8**: Covenant ($20M FY2026) and $25M one-time budget

## Common reasoning traps

- **T1**: Accepting 'wage inflation is the main cause' because the hourly wage rose 9%
- **T2**: Treating e-commerce sales growth as success without counting cannibalisation and fulfillment cost
- **T3**: Recommending deep labor cuts without considering shrink and service effects
- **T4**: Cutting all promotions without considering the competitive response
- **T5**: A list of levers without sizing that does not add up to the 3.0% target
- **T6**: Accepting the e-commerce team's '6.1% contribution margin' claim
- **T7**: Reading 'stores with deeper promotions grew faster' as proof that promotions pay (no control for store mix; the post-event review says 38% of events lose money)

## Key trade-offs

- **X1**: Labor savings vs shrink and service
- **X2**: Promotional margin vs traffic defence against ValuMart
- **X3**: Keeping delivery (owners) vs its economics — reprice/redesign rather than freeze or kill
- **X4**: Store closures (savings) vs lease-exit cost and the board-vote commitment

## Plausible recommendations

- **R1** (defensible: yes): Sized plan led by e-commerce redesign (fees/threshold, shift to click-and-collect, renegotiate delivery), shrink reduction, promotional pruning of losing events and private-label growth, with weak-store closures at lease expiry; labor productivity only where it does not raise shrink.
- **R2** (defensible: conditional): Plan that includes some labor-hour reduction — defensible if targeted (e.g. scheduling, self-checkout) and the shrink risk is acknowledged and sized.
- **R3** (defensible: conditional): Exit home delivery and keep click-and-collect — defensible with the economics, if the owners' commitment is addressed.
- **R4** (defensible: no): The management plan as written (8% hours cut + e-commerce freeze) as the main answer.

## Contradictory evidence

- Hourly wages rose 9.4%, yet labor as a share of sales rose only 0.3 pp because hours per $1,000 fell
- Promotions hurt margin but defend traffic against ValuMart
- Owners want to keep delivery, but it loses money
- E-commerce team's 6.1% margin vs the fulfillment line and cannibalisation

## Criteria for a defensible conclusion

- Diagnosis rests on a quantified bridge, not on the most visible cost
- Actions are sized and add up (roughly) to the 3.0% target, with the covenant in view
- Second-order effects (shrink from labor cuts, traffic from promo cuts) are acknowledged

Alternative conclusions that meet these criteria are acceptable; the list of plausible recommendations is not exhaustive.
