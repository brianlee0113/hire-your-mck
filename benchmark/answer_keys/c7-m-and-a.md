# Answer key — Atlas FieldOps — acquisition decision

> EVALUATOR ONLY. Never include this file in a generation workspace.

## Expected initial-hypothesis handling

**revise**: Full cash purchase is infeasible under $9M floor; staged or partnered acquisition may be defensible only after ARR, churn, synergy and future-earnout diligence.

## Key insights

- **K1** **critical**: Full deal requires $22.00M immediately and leaves $2.00M, violating $9M minimum cash.
- **K2** **critical**: Headline valuation is 3.75× verified ARR, not 2.5×; the seller uses $7.2M bookings including one-off pilots.
- **K3** **critical**: Base cross-sell ARR contributes $1.07M annually net of 80% GM and $0.85M additional support; stress case $-0.21M.
- **K4** **critical**: Customer churn 21% and six-month integration assumptions make the seller synergy case uncertain.
- **K5**: Staged cash expenditure $14.50M just passes the initial cash floor with $9.50M left, but its contingent $7M earnout is a future cash risk.
- **K6**: A nonexclusive partnership can test demand while preserving cash, but price, exclusivity and renewal exposure need negotiation.

## Required calculations

- **Q1**: Cash after full acquisition — expected **$2.00M vs $9.00M floor**, tolerance ±$0.05M
- **Q2**: Cash after staged initial commitment — expected **$9.50M vs $9.00M floor**, tolerance ±$0.05M
- **Q3**: Price / verified ARR — expected **$18.0M / $4.8M = 3.75x**, tolerance ±0.01x
- **Q4**: Annual incremental synergy — expected **base $2.4M×80%−$0.85M=$1.07M; stress $0.8M×80%−$0.85M=$-0.21M**, tolerance ±$0.02M

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **E1** **critical**: Board $9M minimum cash and $15M spendable limit
- **E2** **critical**: Bookings versus ARR definition
- **E3** **critical**: Churn, renewal cohorts and integration cost exposure
- **E4**: Partnership and internal-build alternatives

## Reasoning traps

- **T1**: Taking 2.5x valuation at face value
- **T2**: Assuming routing causes superior renewal
- **T3**: Treating $2.4M cross-sell ARR as immediate cash EBITDA
- **T4**: Ignoring earnout or integration costs in minimum cash tests

## Key tradeoffs

- **X1**: Speed to feature vs capital preservation
- **X2**: Acquisition control vs retention and integration risk
- **X3**: Earnout risk vs financial flexibility

## Plausible recommendations

- **R1**: Start nonexclusive commercial partnership while negotiating an evidence- and cash-gated acquisition option. [defensible: yes]
- **R2**: Pursue staged purchase only if independently verified ARR, retention, integration scope and earnout reserve support it. [defensible: conditional]
- **R3**: Buy for $18M cash plus $4M integration under current limits. [defensible: no]

## Decision requirements

- **D1** **critical**: Can the initial and future cash commitments satisfy minimum liquidity? (acceptable evidence examples: sources-and-uses, cash bridge)
- **D2** **critical**: Is the revenue denominator consistent? (acceptable evidence examples: ARR reconciliation, valuation multiple)
- **D3** **critical**: Is customer retention sensitivity addressed? (acceptable evidence examples: churn scenario, earnout condition)
- **D4** **critical**: Is the strongest non-acquisition path weighed? (acceptable evidence examples: alternative matrix, partnership economics)

## Contradictory evidence


- Sellers multiple uses bookings, finance uses ARR
- Large accounts renew more but also receive more support
- Bookings include nonrenewable pilots and multi-year face value

## Defensibility criteria

- Cash floor governs the full and staged alternatives
- Valuation uses recurring ARR not gross bookings
- Synergies are translated to cash contribution with uncertainty
- Tradeoffs between buy, partner and build are made explicit
