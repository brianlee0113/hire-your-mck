# Answer key · Onsoo Botanicals — US market entry

> **Evaluators only.** Never copy this file into a generation workspace.

**Decision question:** Should Onsoo enter the US market in 2027, and if so, by which route?

## Initial hypothesis in the pack

- Holder: CEO
- Statement: Enter the US in 2027 through an Amazon-led launch; add specialty retail from 2029 once proven.
- Expected handling: **keep_with_conditions**
- Why: The evidence supports the CEO's route over the retail-led route, which breaks the investment cap and the group-margin floor. But Option A meets the four-year payback only if ACoS stays below about 28.6% — barely above the pilot's 28% and below the 32% scaling benchmark — and it reaches about $26M, not $40M, by 2030. A good answer keeps the hypothesis but adds gates and resets the $40M ambition; abandoning the US altogether is defensible only if argued from the payback risk, and adopting the retail-led route is not defensible.

## Key analytical insights

- **K1** (critical): Amazon unit contribution before advertising is $10.72 (44.7% of price); after ads at the pilot's 28% ACoS it is $4.00 per unit. Retail contribution is $7.54 per unit before the large fixed retail costs.
- **K2** (critical): Option B (retail-led) needs peak cumulative investment of about $20.8M even on the planning team's own 4.5 units/door/week (about $25.5M at the 2.6 newcomer median), above the $18M cap, never pays back by 2030, and pushes group operating margin to about 4.5% in 2027, below the 8% floor.
- **K3** (critical): The 4.5 units/door/week assumption is the 'above 20% awareness' benchmark; Onsoo's awareness is 4% (9% among K-beauty buyers), where the median is 2.6 — below the retailer's 4.0 delisting threshold. Mooncrest's exit illustrates the risk.
- **K4** (critical): Option A at the planning ACoS of 25% pays back in 2030 (cumulative $2.40M); at the pilot's 28% it only just pays back ($0.38M); at the 32% scaling benchmark it does not ($-2.32M). Break-even ACoS for payback by 2030 is about 28.6%. A tariff rise to 25% alone turns the pilot case negative ($-0.74M).
- **K5** (critical): No option meets both the $40M ambition and the constraints: A reaches about $26.4M of 2030 revenue; B reaches about $37.6M but breaks the cap. The board has to trade the ambition against the constraints.
- **K6**: Peak investment for A is small ($2.4M at 28% ACoS; $3.9M in the combined downside), so the downside of trying A is bounded; group margin stays above 9%.
- **K7**: Option J (Japan) pays back in 2029 with cumulative $1.8M by 2030 and lower risk, but it is a small, slow-growing market (1.5% a year; Onsoo already holds 3.1%). It is a credible alternative for 2027 given the one-launch bandwidth constraint.
- **K8**: The pilot is not a novelty spike: monthly units nearly doubled (7,400 to 14,000; June run-rate about 168,000 units a year), 4.5 stars, and repeat of 29% is close to the 31% newcomer benchmark — but below Korea's 48%, so Korean repeat rates should not be assumed.
- **K9**: The 1.3× Amazon halo in Option B rests on a cross-brand correlation (0.71) that does not show retail causes Amazon sales: brands that are already successful win more doors (reverse causality / confounding). Without the halo, Option B is even worse.

## Required calculations

- **Q1**: Amazon per-unit contribution before and after advertising — expected: $10.72 before ads; $4.00 after ads at 28% ACoS (tolerance: ±$0.10)
- **Q2**: Retail per-unit contribution — expected: $7.54 (tolerance: ±$0.10)
- **Q3**: Option B cumulative cash / peak investment vs the $18M cap — expected: peak about $20.8M on plan assumptions (tolerance: any estimate clearly above $18M)
- **Q4**: Option A payback / cumulative cash by 2030, with sensitivity to ACoS (and ideally tariff) — expected: 25%: $2.40M; 28%: $0.38M; 32%: $-2.32M (tolerance: ±$0.5M; the qualitative pattern (payback only below ~28–29% ACoS) matters more than exact values)
- **Q5**: Group operating margin check for Option B in 2027 — expected: 4.5% (tolerance: any calculation showing it is below 8%)
- **Q6**: K-beauty US market growth — expected: CAGR 2021–25 17.3%, against 5.1% for all facial skincare; K-beauty share 14.7% in 2025 (tolerance: ±0.5 pp)

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **E1** (critical): Investment cap $18M, payback by end-2030, group margin ≥ 8%, one launch in 2027
- **E2** (critical): Onsoo aided awareness 4% (9% among K-beauty buyers) vs sell-through benchmarks by awareness band
- **E3** (critical): Retailer delists below 4.0 units per door per week after 12 months
- **E4** (critical): Agency ACoS benchmark for scaling brands: median 32% vs planning assumption 25% and pilot 28%
- **E5**: Possible tariff increase from 15% to 25%
- **E6**: 61% of K-beauty buyers buy on Amazon most often; 38% discover on TikTok
- **E7**: Competitor histories (Mooncrest loss; Saebom success but two Amazon-first brands stalled below $5M)
- **E8**: Label complaint (Korean-only text) — a compliance and conversion fix before scaling
- **E9**: Definition of the $5.2B trade-press figure vs Table 1 skincare-only $3.6B

## Common reasoning traps

- **T1**: Sizing from the market ("K-beauty is $3.6B growing 17%; 1% share is $36M") instead of from channel economics
- **T2**: Accepting the 4.5 units/door/week or the 25% ACoS assumptions without checking them against the benchmarks
- **T3**: Using Saebom as proof that Amazon-first works (ignoring the two stalled brands) or Mooncrest as proof retail never works
- **T4**: Assuming Korean repeat rates (48%) or treating the pilot growth rate as permanent
- **T5**: Recommending retail-led to meet the $40M ambition while ignoring that it breaks the cap and the margin floor
- **T6**: Accepting the board member's correlation as proof that retail drives Amazon sales (the 1.3× halo)
- **T7**: Using the trade-press $5.2B (skincare + makeup + hair care) as the skincare market

## Key trade-offs

- **X1**: Ambition ($40M by 2030) vs investment cap, payback and margin floor
- **X2**: Speed/awareness of retail vs capital risk and delisting risk
- **X3**: US upside vs the safer, smaller Japan scale-up (one launch only in 2027)
- **X4**: Advertising intensity (growth) vs contribution and payback

## Plausible recommendations

- **R1** (defensible: yes): Enter through Amazon in 2027 with explicit gates (e.g. ACoS ≤ ~28%, repeat ≥ ~30%, label fix, tariff watch); add retail only when awareness supports ≥ 4 units/door/week; reset the $40M ambition or accept a later date.
- **R2** (defensible: conditional): Prioritise the Japan scale-up in 2027 and keep the cross-border Amazon listing as a low-cost US test — defensible if argued from payback risk and the one-launch constraint, with a clear trigger for a US launch later.
- **R3** (defensible: conditional): Amazon-led entry with a lower fixed-cost base (smaller team, slower brand spend) to protect payback — defensible if numbers are shown.
- **R4** (defensible: no): National specialty-retail launch in 2027 (Option B).
- **R5** (defensible: no): Enter the US 'because the market is large and growing' without channel economics.

## Contradictory evidence

- The planning team's 25% ACoS vs the 32% scaling benchmark
- The retailer's 4.5 units/door/week vs the 2.6 median at Onsoo's awareness
- The $40M ambition vs the investment cap and margin floor
- Saebom's success vs Gaon Lab and Yeoreum stalling (survivorship bias)
- Board member's correlation memo vs what a correlation across brands can show

## Criteria for a defensible conclusion

- Compares the options on the board's own constraints (cap, payback, margin floor), not only on revenue
- Tests the planning assumptions (ACoS, units per door) against the benchmarks in the pack
- States what has to be true for the chosen route to pay back, and what would trigger a change
- Acknowledges that the $40M ambition is not reachable within the constraints on the evidence given

Alternative conclusions that meet these criteria are acceptable; the list of plausible recommendations is not exhaustive.
