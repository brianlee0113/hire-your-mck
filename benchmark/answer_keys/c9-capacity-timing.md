# Answer key — Pelican Packaging — capacity timing

> EVALUATOR ONLY. Never include this file in a generation workspace.

## Expected initial-hypothesis handling

**keep_with_conditions**: Reserving capacity is a modest real option within near-term liquidity while full machine capex currently breaches the cash floor; contract overflow and a financing gate are needed.

## Key insights

- **K1** **critical**: Ordering now leaves $4.00M after approved R&D, below $7.0M floor; slot alone leaves $8.20M.
- **K2** **critical**: Practical accepted capacity is 9.0M, not 10.0M nameplate; forecasts exceed it by 0.6M, 2.2M and 3.4M in 2028–30.
- **K3** **critical**: Existing external molder can cover 2028 and 2029 overflow by volume but not all 2030 forecast overflow or unvalidated specialty SKU.
- **K4** **critical**: Deposit is credited toward $4.8M machine, not an extra cost, and does not remove the future funding need.
- **K5**: The 23% growth correlation between prior investment and sales is not causal evidence.
- **K6**: The 98% contractual fill-rate is monthly; annual capacity arithmetic cannot guarantee it.

## Required calculations

- **Q1**: Cash after immediate full investment — expected **$4.00M vs $7M minimum**, tolerance ±$0.05M
- **Q2**: Cash after forfeitable slot deposit — expected **$8.20M vs $7M minimum; deposit forfeited if cancelled**, tolerance ±$0.05M
- **Q3**: 2028/29 contract-molding premium — expected **$0.07M / $0.26M**, tolerance ±$0.01M
- **Q4**: 2029 line contribution versus turning away incremental overflow (not versus contracting it out) — expected **$0.44M = 2.2M×$0.45−$0.55M before capex**, tolerance ±$0.02M

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **E1** **critical**: Cash minimum after approved R&D
- **E2** **critical**: Nameplate versus practical accepted capacity
- **E3** **critical**: Five-month outsource lead and specialty qualification
- **E4**: Difference between signed minimum and forecast demand

## Reasoning traps

- **T1**: Treating nameplate as sellable capacity
- **T2**: Claiming the $0.6M deposit fixes future financing
- **T3**: Counting deposit twice in equipment outlay
- **T4**: Inferring capacity purchases cause sales growth from peer comparison

## Key tradeoffs

- **X1**: Preserving investment option vs committing capital
- **X2**: Internal capacity vs outsourced premium
- **X3**: Annual capacity vs monthly customer service

## Plausible recommendations

- **R1**: Reserve a slot conditionally, secure contract overflow for 2028, and release full capex only with confirmed volume and liquidity. [defensible: yes]
- **R2**: Defer even the slot if customer demand slips materially or outside molder can meet all constraints more cheaply. [defensible: conditional]
- **R3**: Commit $4.8M now without new financing or R&D adjustment. [defensible: no]

## Decision requirements

- **D1** **critical**: Does full investment fit after R&D and cash reserve? (acceptable evidence examples: cash commitments bridge, covenant test)
- **D2** **critical**: Are all units comparable accepted units? (acceptable evidence examples: practical-capacity test, scrap treatment)
- **D3** **critical**: Can external capacity cover the gap before new machine delivery? (acceptable evidence examples: yearly capacity schedule, lead-time plan)
- **D4** **critical**: What contracts and cash would trigger full investment? (acceptable evidence examples: go/no-go criteria, conditions and timing)

## Contradictory evidence


- 23% more sales for earlier investors is confounded by sector and contracts
- Nameplate utilization vs accepted-unit practical capacity
- Contribution per incremental sale vs outsourced premium are different metrics

## Defensibility criteria

- Respect cash floor and practical capacity
- Credit deposit correctly
- Plan near-term overflow with validation limits
- Set a measurable future investment gate
