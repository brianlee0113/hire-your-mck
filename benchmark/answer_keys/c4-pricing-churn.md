# Answer key · Palisade Docs — pricing while churn rises

> **Evaluators only.** Never copy this file into a generation workspace.

**Decision question:** Should Palisade raise prices in 2027 while customer churn is rising, and if so, how?

## Initial hypothesis in the pack

- Holder: CFO (with the CEO holding the opposite view)
- Statement: Raise list prices 12% on every plan; churn is onboarding, not price ($10.3M).
- Expected handling: **revise**
- Why: Both interpretations are partly right. The SMB churn rise is mostly a mix effect from the 2025 promotion cohort (≈4.9% monthly churn vs 2.1% for other SMB customers; mix effect ≈ 1.0 pp of the 1.1 pp rise) — supporting the CFO — but those customers leave over price when the discount ends and Inkwell has made SMB more price-competitive — supporting the CEO. A good answer differentiates by segment rather than adopting either view wholesale.

## Key analytical insights

- **K1** (critical): Churn rise is concentrated in SMB (2.0% → 3.1%); mid-market rose modestly (0.6% → 0.8%), enterprise flat (0.2%). Revenue-weighted monthly churn rose from 0.83% to 1.19%.
- **K2** (critical): Mix decomposition: non-promo SMB churn 2.0% → 2.1% (rate effect ≈ 0.1 pp); promotion cohort ≈ 4.9% implied; the promotion explains ≈ 1.0 pp of the 1.1 pp SMB increase.
- **K3** (critical): Price is a real churn reason, but mainly for the promotion cohort at discount expiry (52% cite price) vs 18% for non-promo SMB; Inkwell's $9 plan and the win-rate fall (51% → 38%) make SMB price-sensitive.
- **K4** (critical): The $10.3M is overstated: only renewing ARR reprices in 2027 and 60% of enterprise ARR is capped at 7% → about $7.57M before any churn response (SMB $2.52M, mid-market $4.18M, enterprise $0.86M).
- **K5** (critical): Mid-market and enterprise look price-inelastic (low churn, NRR 112%/118%, 2024 test showed no conversion effect, no Inkwell presence) — that is where an increase is safest and worth most.
- **K6**: A 12% increase is revenue-neutral if it causes about 10.7% extra loss of the repriced base. The 2023 SMB +8% increase lost about 3.6% extra customers and netted about 4.1% — but that was before Inkwell.
- **K7**: Activation (≥5 documents in month one) predicts retention; onboarding fixes for the promotion cohort are a separate lever from pricing.
- **K8**: The dashboard's 1.4% → 2.3% matches neither blended logo churn (1.71% → 2.62%) nor revenue-weighted churn (0.83% → 1.19%); a good answer works from the segment table and notes the undefined metric.

## Required calculations

- **Q1**: Segment churn comparison and/or revenue-weighted churn — expected: blended logo churn 1.71% → 2.62%; revenue-weighted 0.83% → 1.19% (tolerance: ±0.1 pp)
- **Q2**: Promotion-cohort churn / mix decomposition — expected: promo ≈ 4.9%; mix ≈ 1.0 pp, rate ≈ 0.1 pp (tolerance: ±0.3 pp)
- **Q3**: Realistic 2027 uplift of a 12% increase — expected: $7.57M (tolerance: ±$0.5M; any correction for renewal timing or caps counts)
- **Q4**: Break-even extra churn / 2023 precedent — expected: 10.7%; 2023 net ≈ 4.1% (tolerance: ±1 pp)

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **E1** (critical): Segment churn table (SMB vs mid-market vs enterprise)
- **E2** (critical): Promotion cohort size, activation and non-promo SMB churn 2.1%
- **E3** (critical): Exit reasons by group (52% vs 18% citing price)
- **E4** (critical): Inkwell $9 plan and SMB win-rate decline
- **E5** (critical): Renewal timing and the 7% enterprise cap
- **E6**: 2023 SMB price-increase history and 2024 mid-market test
- **E7**: NRR by segment
- **E8**: Undefined dashboard churn metric vs the segment table

## Common reasoning traps

- **T1**: Treating blended churn as one number and concluding price sensitivity everywhere (or nowhere)
- **T2**: Taking the $10.3M at face value
- **T3**: Reading exit surveys without splitting by cohort
- **T4**: Generalising the 2024 new-signup test to renewing customers without caveat
- **T5**: Accepting 'onboarding will halve SMB churn' without evidence, cost or timing

## Key trade-offs

- **X1**: ARR uplift vs churn risk, by segment
- **X2**: Competing with Inkwell in SMB (price) vs monetising mid-market/enterprise value
- **X3**: Short-term revenue vs NRR/brand (customer trust at renewal)

## Plausible recommendations

- **R1** (defensible: yes): Differentiated increase: raise mid-market (and enterprise within caps); hold or restructure SMB (grandfather, a lower-priced tier vs Inkwell, fix activation), with a staged test and churn guardrails.
- **R2** (defensible: yes): Raise mid-market/enterprise now; delay SMB changes until the promotion cohort has washed out and activation is fixed.
- **R3** (defensible: conditional): A smaller uniform increase (e.g. 5–7%) with SMB grandfathering — defensible if the segment evidence is addressed.
- **R4** (defensible: no): 12% across the board as proposed.
- **R5** (defensible: no): No increase anywhere because 'churn is rising'.

## Contradictory evidence

- Churn rise looks like price sensitivity (exit surveys, Inkwell) and like a cohort mix effect (promotion, activation)
- 2023 precedent says SMB increases pay off; Inkwell says conditions have changed
- CFO's $10.3M vs renewal timing and contract caps

## Criteria for a defensible conclusion

- Separates the cohort/mix effect from a rate effect
- Recognises both interpretations and reconciles them with the evidence
- Corrects or questions the $10.3M estimate
- Pricing recommendation differs by segment or explains why it should not

Alternative conclusions that meet these criteria are acceptable; the list of plausible recommendations is not exhaustive.
