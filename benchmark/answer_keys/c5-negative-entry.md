# Answer key · Parcelbird — quick-commerce entry

> **Evaluators only.** Never copy this file into a generation workspace.

**Decision question:** Should Parcelbird launch its own quick-commerce grocery delivery service in 2027?

## Initial hypothesis in the pack

- Holder: Founders
- Statement: Enter now: the market grows 40%+ a year and density will make unit economics positive by month 18.
- Expected handling: **reject**
- Why: Contribution per order is negative at benchmark median ($-6.17), at the best observed density ($-3.44) and even combining best density with the best AOV ($-1.76), before customer acquisition and overhead. Break-even AOV at the best density is about $50.37. The founders' +$2.02 relies on every input beating the best observed site. Growth does not fix structurally negative unit economics, and the plan would consume most of the cash. A good answer rejects the hypothesis (or limits it to a capped, capital-light test with kill criteria). The Zipcart fee evidence shows the economics are not impossible at very high density with a fee, which makes a capped, capital-light test (R2) more defensible — but not the three-city plan.

## Key analytical insights

- **K1** (critical): Per-order contribution: median $-6.17 (rider $7.40, store $3.50); best observed density $-3.44; best density + best AOV $-1.76. Negative in every observed case.
- **K2** (critical): The founders' plan (+$2.02) needs AOV $45, 3.5 orders/rider-hour, 1,800 orders/day and 2% promotions — each beyond the best observed site; break-even AOV at best observed density is ≈ $50.37.
- **K3** (critical): The pop-up's contribution was about $-12.57 per order (AOV $31, 12% promotions, 2.1 orders/rider-hour, 280 orders/day) — the 18% weekly order growth is promotion-fuelled top-line growth.
- **K4** (critical): Market growth (CAGR 41.5% 2022–25) coexists with structural losses: both incumbents have negative contribution per order and QuickBasket closed 4 of 11 cities.
- **K5** (critical): Capital: $9.5M of the $12.0M cash to month 18 would leave ≈ $2.5M, far below 12 months of runway (≈ $4.8M at the current $0.4M/month burn) — breaking the runway constraint even if the plan works; no follow-on funding expected.
- **K6**: Customer economics are weak: CAC $38 and 22% month-3 retention; at negative contribution per order no customer ever pays back.
- **K7**: The better path: grow the 81%-gross-margin software business — 37 grocers in pipeline (9 late stage, ~$96K ACV), several wanting their own fast delivery, i.e. sell the picks and shovels.
- **K8**: Stated intent (44% weekly) is not demand at a price; and rider pay rules are a further downside risk.
- **K9** (critical): Engages with the evidence that partly supports the founders: with a $3.99 fee and 1% promotions, a site at the best observed density would make about $2.25 per order, but a benchmark-median site still loses about $-0.48 (about $-1.05 if volume falls 14% as at Zipcart), before acquisition cost; Parcelbird's own pop-up ran far below median density. Positive unit economics are possible only at density no Parcelbird site has shown, and the runway constraint is broken regardless.

## Required calculations

- **Q1**: Contribution per order at benchmark median — expected: $-6.17 (tolerance: ±$0.30)
- **Q2**: Contribution at best observed density (and/or best AOV) — expected: $-3.44 / $-1.76 (tolerance: ±$0.30)
- **Q3**: Pop-up contribution per order — expected: $-12.57 (tolerance: ±$0.50)
- **Q4**: Cash / runway after the plan — expected: $12.0M − $9.5M = $2.5M, about 6 months at $0.4M/month vs 12 months required (tolerance: qualitative)
- **Q5**: Market CAGR — expected: 41.5% (tolerance: ±1 pp)

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **E1** (critical): Per-order benchmark inputs and the best observed site
- **E2** (critical): Incumbent losses and QuickBasket city exits
- **E3** (critical): Cash, burn and runway constraint; no follow-on funding
- **E4** (critical): Pop-up test: low AOV, high promotions, low density
- **E5**: CAC $38 and 22% month-3 retention
- **E6**: Software pipeline (37 grocers, 9 late stage)
- **E7**: Rider minimum-pay rules under consideration

## Common reasoning traps

- **T1**: 'The market is growing fast, so enter'
- **T2**: 'Scale/density will fix the economics' without checking the best observed case
- **T3**: Taking the founders' assumptions or the survey at face value
- **T4**: Treating promotion-fuelled order growth as validation
- **T5**: Ignoring the runway constraint and opportunity cost to the software business
- **T6**: Either ignoring the Zipcart fee evidence, or treating it as proof the plan works without checking density, the volume loss and the runway

## Key trade-offs

- **X1**: Growth market upside vs structurally negative unit economics
- **X2**: Becoming an operator vs staying a software supplier (focus, capital)
- **X3**: Learning from a test vs consuming runway

## Plausible recommendations

- **R1** (defensible: yes): Do not launch; focus capital on the software business and sell to grocers launching fast delivery.
- **R2** (defensible: conditional): A small, capped, capital-light test (e.g. with a grocer partner's existing store, no own dark stores) with explicit kill criteria tied to contribution per order — defensible only if it shows why it cannot become the founders' plan by stealth.
- **R3** (defensible: no): Launch in three cities as planned.
- **R4** (defensible: no): Launch in one city 'to prove density' without showing that even the best observed density loses money.

## Contradictory evidence

- Fast market growth and 18% weekly pop-up growth vs negative unit economics
- Survey intent (44% weekly) vs retention (22% at month three)
- Founders' model vs benchmark and best observed inputs
- Zipcart's positive contribution in its densest city (after a fee and promo cut) vs negative economics elsewhere

## Criteria for a defensible conclusion

- Computes per-order contribution under realistic (benchmark/best observed) inputs
- Separates market growth from profitability
- Checks the cash/runway constraint
- Offers the software alternative or an explicitly capped test

Alternative conclusions that meet these criteria are acceptable; the list of plausible recommendations is not exhaustive.
