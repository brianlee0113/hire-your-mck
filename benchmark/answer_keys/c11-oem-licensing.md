## Expected initial-hypothesis handling

**revise**: The signing payment is a creditable advance, not extra royalty revenue; certification and direct channel overlap make unconditional exclusivity premature. A gated pilot or contingent limited exclusivity can be defensible.

## Key insights

- **K1** **critical**: The $2M upfront is credited against royalties, so 2027 $1.5M of nominal royalties does not add $1.5M more cash; 2028 cash receipts are reduced by the remaining $0.5M.
- **K2** **critical**: 2028 royalty after $80/device and $0.9M fixed OEM support yields $1.30M before the direct-channel opportunity cost.
- **K3** **critical**: At 65% OEM volume, 2028 standalone partner contribution is $0.53M before direct-channel tradeoffs.
- **K4** **critical**: Direct ARR at risk $1.8M at 82% GM represents up to $1.48M of potential incremental gross profit, not necessarily all cannibalized.
- **K5** **critical**: 98.8% lab uptime is below 99.5% production acceptance; exclusivity effective at signature precedes necessary certification.
- **K6**: Nonexclusive pilot and milestone-triggered exclusivity preserve option value and contract audit rights.

## Decision-relevant evidence

- **E1** **critical**: Minimum guarantee credited against royalty payments
- **E2** **critical**: Product certification before irreversible exclusivity
- **E3** **critical**: OEM support costs and possible direct channel displacement
- **E4**: Audit, termination and IP provisions

## Reasoning traps

- **T1**: Adding a recoupable advance to nominal royalties twice
- **T2**: Taking forecast units as audited shipments
- **T3**: Attributing peer growth causally to exclusivity
- **T4**: Ignoring support, certification and existing direct contracts

## Key tradeoffs

- **X1**: Upfront liquidity vs long-term channel control
- **X2**: Exclusivity leverage vs direct opportunity costs
- **X3**: OEM upside vs certification and data risk

## Plausible recommendations

- **R1**: Negotiate a nonexclusive pilot and contingent exclusivity only after certification, shipment proof and audit rights. [defensible: yes]
- **R2**: Accept limited geographic or time-based exclusivity with enforceable royalty minima and exit gates if incremental economics exceed channel loss. [defensible: conditional]
- **R3**: Sign the three-year unconditional North American exclusive contract immediately based on forecast volume and gross royalty dashboard. [defensible: no]

## Decision requirements

- **D1** **critical**: Are the advance and credited royalties counted only once? (acceptable evidence examples: royalty schedule, cash timing reconciliation)
- **D2** **critical**: Does exclusivity wait for required certification? (acceptable evidence examples: regulatory and technical gate, timeline)
- **D3** **critical**: Does partner economics include cost and channel opportunity cost? (acceptable evidence examples: incremental contribution case, overlap sensitivity)
- **D4**: Are enforceable partner performance exits considered? (acceptable evidence examples: audit and exit thresholds, nonexclusive alternative)

## Contradictory evidence


- Bookable future devices vs paid shipped robots
- Correlation of OEM exclusivity with stronger competitors growth
- 100% royalty margin excludes service costs and direct opportunity cost

## Defensibility criteria

- No double counting of signing advance and royalties
- Evaluate net support costs and potential lost direct contribution
- Certification and contract audit/termination rights are binding
- Compare a real nonexclusive alternative and specify decision gates
