# Parcelbird Now – board deck

**File:** `Parcelbird_Now_board_deck.pptx` (17 slides, 16:9)

**Answer:** Do not launch Parcelbird Now in 2027. Put the cash into the software business, which can reach profitability with current cash, and keep quick commerce as an option that is reopened only if set triggers are met.

## Storyline (pyramid)
1. Title
2. Executive summary: the answer, three supporting points, and the decisions requested
3. Hypothesis test: the founders' "yes" was tested against 5 conditions and only market growth holds
4–9. **It would lose money:** market, unit economics, the founders' plan, sensitivity, the pop-up, customers
10. **We cannot afford it:** runway and capital
11–12. **Software is the better use of the cash:** options compared, software path to profitability
13. Recommendation, next steps and re-entry triggers
14. Risks, assumptions and uncertainties
15–17. Appendix: per-order calculations, runway and software-model calculations, interpretation choices

## Choices made without the briefing team (also on slide 17)
- "Contribution" means per-order revenue minus variable costs, before site fixed costs. The site-level result is also shown.
- It is unclear whether the $9.5M cash need includes today's $0.4M/month software burn. Both readings are shown, and both break the 12-month runway rule.
- The "best observed" column is treated as an upper bound, because its AOV and density come from different sites.
- The pack gives no figure for rider minimum pay, so the uplifts (+10%, +20%, and +$1/hr) are illustrative.
- The software path is an indicative model. Opex is derived from ARR and burn and held flat apart from $0.8M of hires, revenue is taken as equal to ARR, and gross margin is 81%.
- Runway is cash divided by the current monthly burn. The plan's spending is spread evenly over 18 months because the pack does not say how it is phased.
