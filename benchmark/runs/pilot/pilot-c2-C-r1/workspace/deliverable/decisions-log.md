## Planning assumptions (all shown on the slides and in the workbook)

- Every lever is sized at FY2025 volumes, with no market growth.
- Promotions return to the FY2023 share (24%) in two steps. The second step happens only if traffic holds in test stores. The $10.5M is an upper bound.
- Private label keeps its FY2023–25 pace of 0.75 percentage points a year.
- Shrink: stores in Q1 and Q2 reach the Q3 level of 2.4% through fixed routines. Hours are cut 8% only in the best-staffed quarter of stores, taken as a quarter of the labor bill.
- The tested delivery fees are applied chain-wide, and every lost online sale is counted as lost.
- The closure gain is split between the six stores and the three by store count. Store-level EBITDA should be confirmed before the vote.
- The promotion, private-label and shrink gains are not counted on the sales that leave with the closures. They appear as a separate "sales base" step.

## Open points to flag to the board

1. **Fiscal-year timing.** The pack treats FY2026 as the first plan year and sets the covenant on it. Under both usual fiscal-year conventions, FY2026 has ended by February 2027. The deck follows the pack, but footnotes the conflict. The covenant should be tested on FY2026 actuals.
2. **2027 minimum wage rise.** It is not in the base case. If it passes through to the whole wage bill, it costs up to $7.3M a year and takes the FY2028 margin to about 2.7%.
3. **Reserve levers that are not sized** because the pack has no data: winning back vendor funding (it fell from 55% to 46% of markdowns) and a second step on delivery fees.
