=== Slide 2 ===
[Title] Harlow can return to a 3.0% margin by FY2028 by reversing what caused the decline, not by cutting store hours
- EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- The problem
- Operating margin fell from 3.4% to 1.0% in two years. Operating income fell from $47.9M to $15.3M while sales grew 6%.
- 1.0% → 3.0%
- The cause
- Heavier promotions (−0.70 pts), e-commerce fulfillment (−0.84) and higher shrink (−0.50) explain 2.0 of the 2.4-point fall. Store labor explains 0.3: wages rose 9.4%, but hours per $ of sales fell 6%.
- Operating margin, FY2025 actual → FY2028 plan
- The draft plan falls short
- Cutting store hours 8% saves $14.5M on paper, but the stores with the fewest hours already have the most shrink. The draft plan reaches 1.1–1.8% in FY2028, not 3.0%.
- ~$24M
- Recommendation
- Five moves worth ~$30M a year by FY2028: reset promotions, rebalance labor to cut shrink, price delivery closer to cost, exit the 9 loss-making stores, grow private label. After the 2027 wage rise, operating income reaches ~$42M, a 3.0% margin.
- FY2026 operating income outlook vs $20M covenant
- Act first
- In the next 90 days: roll out the tested delivery fee, drop loss-making promotions, replace the hours cut with a rebalance, hold the delivery contract rate, give notice on 6 expiring leases. FY2026 operating income: ~$24M against the $20M covenant.
- $7.5M
- One-time cost identified, of the $25M allowed
- The risk
- 3.0% needs every move to deliver. The moves within management’s control reach ~2.3%. The rest depends on vendor talks, a second delivery-price test and a board vote on 3 stores.
- Source: Harlow Fresh Markets briefing pack (FY2023–FY2025). Fiscal years end in January. Plan values are annual run-rates in constant FY2025 terms; see appendix for sizing.

=== Slide 14 ===
[Title] Choices we made where the briefing pack is silent
- APPENDIX B
- Timing
- The pack has actuals to FY2025 and treats FY2026 as the first plan year (the capital window, the draft hours cut and the covenant all start there). We do the same. If FY2026 (ending January 2027) has already closed when the board meets, replace the covenant outlook with the actual and discuss it with the lender. The FY2027–28 plan does not change.
- Constant terms
- The plan is sized at FY2025 prices and volumes, with no market growth. Routine cost inflation is assumed to be covered by price. The 2027 minimum-wage step is the only headwind we model explicitly.
- Cannibalization
- The 70% of online sales that would otherwise be made in-store are assumed to earn the same margin in either channel. The survey (n = 2,300) is self-reported.
- What we have not counted
- One-time costs other than the $7.5M lease exits (closures, private-label development, shrink tools). Finance should cost these within the $17.5M left.
- Any renegotiated cut in the delivery partner’s rate (each 5% ≈ $0.5M).
- Theft, 35% of shrink, beyond what better receiving catches.
- Further ValuMart openings, which would add to the pressure on traffic.
- What to validate before the plan is locked
- Whether the 14-pt promotional gap is measured after vendor funding.
- Labor hours and sales by quartile, and by each of the 9 weak stores.
- Lease-end dates for the 6 stores, which set when their savings start.
- Source: Harlow Fresh Markets briefing pack (FY2023–FY2025). Fiscal years end in January.
