=== Slide 2 ===
[Title] Executive summary
- Parcelbird should not launch Parcelbird Now in 2027: its profit rests on results no site has reached, and the cash does more in the software business
- 1
- Quick commerce is growing fast, but no operator has made it pay, even at national scale
- –	US quick-commerce grocery sales nearly tripled from 2022 to 2025, to $5.1B
- –	Zipcart loses $1.90 and QuickBasket $3.40 per order; QuickBasket closed 4 of 11 cities
- –	Zipcart's densest city turned positive only with a $3.99 fee, and lost 14% of orders
- p. 4–6
- 2
- The founders' plan makes money only on four inputs that beat the best site Parcelbird's software runs
- –	At benchmark medians a dark store loses $6.17 per order and needs a $63 basket to break even
- –	The plan assumes a $45 basket and 1,800 orders a day; the best site reached $42 and 1,400
- –	Each Columbus customer still ordering after three months cost about $173 to win
- p. 8–11
- 3
- Even if everything goes to plan, the launch breaks the board's 12-month runway rule
- –	The $9.5M the plan needs leaves $2.5M at month 18, about six months of runway
- –	At benchmark medians nine stores would lose about $18M a year, more than all the cash
- p. 13, 14
- 4
- Scaling the software is the better use of the cash, and it serves grocers who want fast delivery
- –	Nine late-stage grocers would add $864K of ARR, for hires costing $0.8M a year
- –	Return to quick commerce only once a site proves the economics and funding exists
- p. 16–18
