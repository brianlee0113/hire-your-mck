=== Slide 2 ===
[Title] Executive summary
- Keep the East DC open in 2027 and optimize it: closing saves only $0.1M a year while breaking the 92% two-day commitment and Central's capacity
- 1
- Closing East saves $0.1M a year, not $1.3M, and would lose $650K before the East lease ends in 2029
- –	Higher freight from Central and extra damage absorb 92% of the fixed-cost saving the CFO's slide counts
- –	With 10% Eastern growth the 2028 saving falls to zero, so the $0.75M exit fee is never recovered
- p. 4, 5
- 2
- Closure also breaks the board's hard limits: the 92% two-day contract KPI and Central's permitted capacity
- –	Network two-day delivery would fall to 90.3% of orders, while keeping East delivers 95.1%
- –	Central would run at 100% of its permit in 2027 and about 8% above it in 2028
- –	Fixing service or capacity would cost more than the saving or the $0.9M one-time budget allows
- p. 7–9
- 3
- Retaining and optimizing East (Option K) is the cheapest design that meets every commitment
- –	Option K serves Eastern orders for $2.6M a year, compared with $2.8M for closure and $3.05M for the cross-dock
- –	Option K is the only design that passes service, capacity, budget and reversibility tests
- –	The cross-dock's 93% rests on a 12,000-order pilot without holiday weeks, and its fee terms are unclear
- p. 11–13
- 4
- Approve Option K now and settle East's future at the 2029 lease end, after a holiday-peak cross-dock test
- –	Option K starts in the first half of 2027; the board takes the 2029 lease decision in mid-2028
- –	Only a cross-dock fee that proves to be a spending floor, plus proven peak service, would change the answer
- p. 15, 16
