=== Slide 2 ===
[Title] Yes, raise prices in 2027, but only for mid-market and enterprise. Hold SMB, test it, and fix the promotion cohort with onboarding, not price
- EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- WHAT THE EVIDENCE SAYS
- WHAT WE RECOMMEND
- Churn is rising in one place. About 90% of the SMB rise (2.0% → 3.1% a month) comes from the 2,100 customers who joined on the 2025 half-price promotion. Other SMB customers moved from 2.0% to 2.1%.
- The promotion cohort is an onboarding problem. Only 41% got started properly (72% for other cohorts), and most leavers went within two months of the discount ending.
- Mid-market and enterprise can take an increase. Their NRR is 112% and 118%, a +10% list test had no significant effect on conversion, and Inkwell is absent above 200 seats.
- SMB is the open question. Inkwell sells at $9 against our $15. A 12% SMB increase adds about $1.2M if customers react as they did in 2023, and nothing if they react twice as strongly.
- The CFO's $10.3M is $7.6M before any churn response, because only part of the ARR renews in 2027 and enterprise increases are capped.
- Mid-market: +10% list price at renewal (the tested level).
- Enterprise: up to the 7% cap where contracts cap increases; up to +10% elsewhere.
- SMB: hold $15 in H1 and split-test +8% in Q1. Extend it to H2 renewals only if churn holds.
- Promotion cohort: no increase. Run an onboarding programme aimed at returning it to normal SMB churn.

=== Slide 13 ===
[Title] How the main figures were calculated
- APPENDIX A · CALCULATIONS
- CFO headline
- 12% × $86.0M = $10.3M. This assumes every dollar of ARR reprices in 2027 at the full 12%.
- CFO before churn response
- SMB 12% × $21.0M = $2.52M; mid-market 12% × 85% × $41.0M = $4.18M; enterprise renewing $9.6M (40% of $24.0M): 60% capped at 7% = $0.40M, plus 40% at 12% = $0.46M. Total $7.57M.
- SMB churn response
- 2023: +8% gave +0.5 pts for six months, then +0.1 pts. Scaled linearly to 12%: +0.75 pts × 6 months + 0.15 pts × 6 months = 5.4% of customers lost, × $23.5M repriced ARR = $1.27M (×2 = $2.54M).
- Recommended price ARR
- Mid-market 10% × $34.85M = $3.49M. Enterprise: $5.76M capped at 7% = $0.40M, plus $3.84M at 10% = $0.38M. Total $4.27M.
- SMB H2 option
- +8% on roughly half of regular SMB ARR (renewing in H2): 8% × 50% × $16.6M ≈ $0.66M. Regular SMB ARR = $21.0M − $4.4M cohort.
- Existing-customer bridge
- $21.0M × 94% + $41.0M × 112% + $24.0M × 118% = $93.98M (+$7.98M), plus price $4.27M = $98.25M (+14.2%). 15% target = $98.9M, so the gap is $0.65M.
- Break-evens
- Gain p is cancelled when the share of customers lost in the year reaches p ÷ (1 + p). Mid-market at 10%: 9.1% a year ≈ +0.8 pts a month. SMB at 8%: 7.4% ≈ +0.6 pts. SMB at 12%: 10.7%, about twice the 2023 response.
- Promotion-cohort value
- 12-month retention at 4.9% a month = 55%; at 2.1% = 78%. Difference × $4.4M ≈ $1.0M.
- SMB test size
- About 1,900 customers per arm for three months at a 2.1% base rate gives a standard error of about 0.27 pts on the difference. A 0.6-pt reaction is about 2.2 standard errors (detectable); a 0.3-pt reaction is about 1.1 (not). This assumes most test customers renew within Q1, which needs monthly plans; fewer renewals means a weaker test.
- Source: Palisade briefing pack (2026), synthetic benchmark data.
