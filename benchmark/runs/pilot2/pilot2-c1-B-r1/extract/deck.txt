=== Slide 2 ===
[Title] Onsoo should enter the US in 2027 with an Amazon-led launch: it is the only route that fits the board's financial rules, but it needs gates because payback depends on ad cost
- 1
- Enter the US in 2027, rather than scale up Japan
- US K-beauty skincare is $3.6B, growing 17% a year (category: 5%), and 41% of it sells on Amazon.
- The pilot shows demand: 64,800 units in six months at 4.5 stars, a $4.0M annualised run-rate, and 29% repeat (benchmark 31%).
- Amazon-led gives $26.4M of US revenue in 2030 vs $7.0M of extra revenue from the Japan scale-up, with peak cash of only $1.9–3.2M.
- Lead with Amazon, not a 600-door specialty-retail launch
- 2
- Amazon-led passes all three financial tests: peak cash –$1.9M (cap $18M), payback in 2030, group margin never below 9.5%.
- Retail-led fails all three on its own numbers: peak cash –$20.8M, no payback by 2030, group margin 4.5% in 2027.
- Its key inputs do not hold: at Onsoo's 4% awareness, newcomers sell a median 2.6 units/door/week (plan: 4.5; delisting below 4.0), and the 1.3× halo rests on a correlation.
- 3
- Launch with conditions, because three parts of the CEO's plan need changing
- Payback holds only if ACoS ≤ 28.6%. At the 32% benchmark for brands this size, cash is still –$2.3M at end-2030, so set a 28% ACoS ceiling and hold a gate in March 2028.
- Retail from 2029 at the planned cost would break payback and the margin floor. Enter retail after payback and above 20% awareness (not 15%).
- No modeled route reaches $40M by 2030 inside the cash cap and payback rule. Keep the rules and move the $40M date to about 2032.
- Decision for March 2027: approve the Amazon-led launch with a $4M cash envelope, a 28% ACoS ceiling and a gate in March 2028; defer specialty retail; move the $40M ambition to about 2032.
- Source: Onsoo briefing pack (Tables 1–9, survey Sept 2026); team analysis. Cash figures are pre-tax, cumulative US cash flow 2027–2030 incl. inventory.

=== Slide 10 ===
[Title] The 1.3× Amazon "halo" behind retail-led rests on a correlation; to pay for retail's costs the halo would need to be 2.1–3.4× from 2028
- 1  Enter in 2027
- 2  Lead with Amazon
- 3  Launch with conditions
- Why a 0.71 correlation does not show that retail drives Amazon
- Reverse causation: retailers sign brands that already sell. Saebom reached ~$30M on Amazon first and then added 300 doors in 2025.
- Common cause: older, bigger, better-funded brands have both more doors and more Amazon sales.
- Survivorship: the sample is 30 brands sold in the US today. Failures such as Mooncrest are probably not in it.
- Size of effect: r = 0.71 means r² ≈ 0.50 shared variance. It says nothing about direction or about how big a 1.3× uplift would be.
- Customer evidence points the other way: only 17% of K-beauty buyers found their last new brand in a store (38% on TikTok), and 22% buy at specialty retail most often.
- Amazon multiple needed for retail to cover its own costs

- Dashed orange line = the 1.3× halo assumed in Option B (a 30% uplift)

[Title] Appendix: interpretation choices and key modeling assumptions
- Choices made where we would have asked
- "$40M by 2030" is read as a 2030 revenue target and treated as an ambition. The cash cap, payback and margin floor are treated as hard constraints.
- US revenue = what Onsoo receives: $24 on Amazon and $14 wholesale for retail, not shelf value.
- Retail volumes run for the full year from 2027, as the planning team implies; no ramp-up.
- Tariff stress applies 25% duty to all four years (conservative, since the decision is mid-2027).
- Listings move from the cross-border seller to Onsoo's own operation inside the $0.8M setup cost.
- Stall case = Amazon flat at 200k units ($4.8M) a year, consistent with peers below $5M.
- Cash envelope $4M is our proposal, covering the worst non-stall stress case (–$3.9M).
- Modeling conventions
- Pre-tax US cash flow; constant FX (KRW 1,380/$); no terminal value.
- Cash = units × unit contribution – fixed costs – one-time costs – change in inventory.
- Inventory = 4 months of landed cost (ex-factory + freight + duty): $4.575 a unit on Amazon, $4.775 in retail.
- Door fixtures ($1,100 per new door) expensed in the year the door opens.
- Group margin = (ex-US plan revenue × 11% + US operating result) ÷ (ex-US revenue + US revenue). Inventory is excluded from the margin.
- Hero-SKU unit economics applied to all SKUs, as the planning team does.
- Japan (Option J): incremental revenue × 30% – $0.8M fixed – $1.0M one-time; no working-capital data provided.
- Peak cash = the most negative cumulative cash position, 2027–30.
- All inputs come from the briefing pack; no outside data used. Figures are synthetic benchmark data.
