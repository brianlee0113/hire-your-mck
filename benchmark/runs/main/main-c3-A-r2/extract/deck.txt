=== Slide 2 ===
[Title] Launch Copilot in Q2 2027 as part of a new Pro tier at +$12 per seat. A $149 add-on would earn less, and Copilot free in Core would breach the margin floor
- EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- Launch. The competitive threat is real, though smaller than claimed
- Decisions requested today
- Routewise put AI scheduling in its Pro plan, and since then our win rate in deals against them has fallen from 44% to 37%. At most that is ~110 deals, or $0.6M of new ARR a year, not the $1.9M the Sales memo implies.
- In the beta, jobs per dispatcher-hour rose 11%, against 2% for matched accounts. The beta accounts volunteered, so this is an upper bound.
- 1. Approve a Q2 2027 launch of Pro: $91 per seat ($79 Core + $12), including Copilot with 600 AI requests per seat per month, pooled per account.
- 2. Reject the $149 add-on and the free-in-Core option.
- 3. Assign 14 engineers to Copilot for Q1–Q2, and re-baseline the mobile rewrite two quarters later with a fixed date.
- 4. Approve the Q1 price test and the March go/no-go checkpoint.
- Package it as a Pro tier. The $8M add-on case rests on the survey
- When beta users actually hit a $149 paywall, 14% bought, against 46% in the survey. That means at most $2.5M of add-on ARR.
- A flat $149 attracts heavy users: their inference costs eat 41% of the price, and 88% for the top accounts. A per-seat Pro tier grows with account size and matches Routewise. Base case: ~$2.2M ARR, +3.8 NRR points and a 76.6% company gross margin.
- Including Copilot free in Core would take gross margin to 72–74%, below the 75% floor.
- Guardrails. Protect mobile and test before we scale
- Launching first delays the mobile rewrite by two quarters, which costs ~$0.5M of churn we could have avoided. Holding Copilot back until mobile ships would forgo ~$1.1M or more of Copilot ARR.
- Go or no-go in March: if a random-sample Pro offer points to adoption below 15%, the 14 engineers go back to mobile.
- Source: Dispatch Copilot briefing pack (fictional data prepared for this exercise). Ranges and assumptions: slides 10, 14 and 15.

=== Slide 9 ===
[Title] Pro at $91 per seat (Core plus $12) includes Copilot and a pooled allowance of 600 AI requests per seat
- RECOMMENDED PACKAGE
- Element
- Recommendation
- Why
- Plan
- A new Pro tier: Core plus Dispatch Copilot (schedule proposals, quote drafts, call summaries). Core stays at $79.
- An opt-in upgrade avoids raising the Core price (price drives 22% of churn), and it mirrors Routewise’s packaging.
- Price
- +$12 per office seat per month ($91 in total).
- Revenue grows with account size, which is what drives usage. A typical 6.2-seat account pays $74 a month, half the $149 add-on.
- Allowance
- 600 AI requests per seat per month, pooled across the account.
- That is 35% above the beta mean (444 per seat), and it holds worst-case inference to $5.40 per seat, or 45% of the uplift.
- Overage
- $20 per extra 1,000 requests (about 2.2× today’s cost).
- The heaviest accounts (the top 10% average 14,500 requests a month) pay for what they use rather than eating into margin.
- New business
- Pro becomes the lead plan in every deal Routewise contests.
- It neutralises the “AI included” comparison, worth up to $0.64M of new ARR a year.
- Inference contract
- Renegotiate in H2 2027, ahead of the December 2027 expiry.
- List prices fell 35% in 12 months. We keep the savings as margin and hold the price.
- Our choice, to be validated: $12 is the only per-seat price in the pack, and the 600-request allowance and $20 overage are our proposals. The Q1 test adds a $15 arm to check whether customers would pay more.
- Source: Dispatch Copilot briefing pack (fictional data prepared for this exercise). Allowance and overage levels are recommendations, not tested figures.

=== Slide 12 ===
[Title] Four uncertainties could change the answer. Each has a test and a trigger
- RISKS
- Uncertainty
- Why it matters
- How we test it
- Trigger and action
- Will demand hold beyond volunteers?
- All adoption and productivity data come from volunteer accounts, which are larger than average.
- Q1: offer Pro to a random sample of non-beta accounts at $12, with a $15 arm, and measure conversion and usage.
- Below 15% implied adoption: pause the launch and move the engineers back to mobile.
- Usage and inference cost
- The top 10% of accounts make 58% of requests, and the inference price is fixed only until December 2027.
- Track requests per seat each month and renegotiate the contract in H2 2027.
- Mean above 600 per seat, or Copilot margin below 55%: tighten the allowance or raise the overage price.
- Is Routewise’s AI the cause?
- Our win-rate drop coincided with Routewise’s 20%-off first-year promotion.
- Q1 win-loss interviews that separate AI from price. Track the win rate after launch.
- No recovery toward 44% by Q4 2027: respond on price and promotion rather than adding more AI.
- Cost of the mobile delay
- Mobile is the top churn reason, and its rewrite slips by two quarters.
- Use a re-baselined date and track churn by reason each month.
- Mobile-related churn rising: bring the engineers back as soon as the launch ships.
- What would change our recommendation
- Mobile first: the Q1 test implies Pro adoption below 15%, or engineering confirms the rewrite would end most mobile churn.
- A higher Pro price: the $15 arm converts at least 80% as well as the $12 arm. At that point $15 earns the same or more ARR (12 ÷ 15 = 0.8).
- Not launching: random-sample accounts show no productivity gain and win-loss work shows Routewise’s promotion, not its AI, drove the losses.
