=== Slide 2 ===
[Title] EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- Executive summary: do not launch Parcelbird Now in 2027. Grow the software business, which can reach break-even on today’s cash
- Answer: No. Parcelbird Now loses money on every combination of inputs seen so far, and it would break the board’s 12-month runway floor even if everything went to plan. Put the cash into the software business instead, including selling fast delivery to grocers, and come back to the operator idea only if the triggers on page 16 are met.
- 1 The market is growing fast, but nobody is making money from it yet
- • US quick-commerce GMV grew 42% a year from 2022 to 2025 ($1.8B to $5.1B).
- • Both incumbents lose money (EBITDA −26% and −46% of revenue). The regional player, QuickBasket, closed 4 of its 11 cities in 2025.
- 2 A Parcelbird dark store loses money unless every input beats the best value observed so far
- • At the 14-site benchmark median a store loses $6.17 per order after site costs. Taking the best observed value for every input still loses $1.76.
- • The founders’ +$2.02 needs four inputs above anything observed. The Columbus pop-up was below the median on all four, and its customers do not order often enough to repay the $38 acquisition cost.
- 3 The launch breaks the board’s funding rules even if it goes to plan
- • Spending $9.5M by month 18 leaves at most $2.5M, about 6 months of today’s burn. The board’s floor is 12 months, or at least $4.8M.
- • At median economics, nine stores would lose about $18M a year. A one-store pilot (about $3.4M) would breach the floor by month 6.
- 4 Growing the software business is the better route to profit, and to quick commerce
- • With $0.8M a year of new hires, the software business breaks even in about 26 months, with cash bottoming out at $5.4M. It stays above the floor as long as ARR grows at least 34% a year.
- • There are 37 grocers in the pipeline ($3.6M of annual contract value), and several want fast delivery from their own stores, which Parcelbird can sell them as software.
- Decisions requested (page 14): turn down the launch and the pilot; approve $0.8M a year of software hires; fund a fast-delivery offer for grocers; adopt the reopening triggers.
- Source: Parcelbird briefing pack (synthetic benchmark data); team analysis. Calculations on pages 4–12 and in the appendix. 2
