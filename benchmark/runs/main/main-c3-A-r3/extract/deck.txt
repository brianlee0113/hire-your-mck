=== Slide 2 ===
[Title] Launch Copilot in Q2 2027 inside a Pro tier at +$12 per seat, with a usage allowance, and make the mobile rewrite the next commitment
- EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- Launch?
- Yes, in Q2 2027. Routewise has bundled AI scheduling since Sept 2026, and our win rate against them fell from 44% to 37%. Holding back costs up to ~$1.1M ARR a year in lost deals and AI churn. The price is a two-quarter delay to the mobile rewrite, which is a real cost (slide 8).
- Packaging
- Include Copilot in a new Pro plan; no standalone add-on. This matches how Routewise sells AI. It also ties price to account size: a 3-seat shop pays $36 a month, not $149. And it avoids an add-on that mostly attracts the heaviest users.
- Price
- Pro = Core + $12 per seat per month ($91), with 600 AI requests per seat per month pooled; overage $20 per 1,000. The allowance keeps company gross margin at or above 75% even if every Pro account uses Copilot heavily.
- Value
- $1.2M–$2.9M expansion ARR in year one (14–33% of accounts move to Pro). That adds +2.1 to +5.0 NRR points. The CPO's $8M is not supported: only 14% of beta accounts bought at $149, against the 46% who said they would in the survey.
- NRR 105%
- Copilot alone is unlikely to get there; Copilot plus the mobile rewrite does, even in the low case. Mobile reliability drives 27% of churn, against 6% for AI. The 14 engineers go back to mobile as soon as Copilot launches.
- Guardrails
- Review at end of Q3 2027: ≥8% of accounts on Pro, inference cost ≤$5.40 per Pro seat, win rate against Routewise recovering, productivity gains holding outside the volunteer accounts, and the mobile rewrite restarted. Renegotiate the inference contract before Dec 2027.
- Source: Brightwork briefing pack (beta results, paywall test, survey, win/loss and churn data). Calculations in appendix A1.

=== Slide 4 ===
[Title] The $8M first-year forecast relies on stated intent from a skewed survey. Actual buying in the paywall test points to $2.5M at most for a $149 add-on
- TESTING THE CPO PROPOSAL
- Share of accounts buying Copilot at $149/account/month, and implied ARR
- Why the survey overstates demand
[Chart: BAR_CLUSTERED]
  categories: Survey: “likely to buy” → $8.1M ARR; Paywall test: bought → ≤ $2.5M ARR
  series 's': 0.46; 0.14
- Self-selected: only 6% of admins replied, and they skew to larger accounts (9.4 vs 6.2 seats).
- Intent, not purchase: “likely” or “very likely” counts soft yeses.
- Behaviour beats stated intent: when asked to pay, 86% of beta accounts said no.
- The beta's other results are also from volunteers: renewal of 97% vs 93% and +11% productivity show correlation, not proof that Copilot causes them.
- Even 14% is likely a ceiling. The test ran on beta volunteers, who are larger (8.1 seats) and more active than the base and had already used the product for 8 weeks. If base adoption is half the test rate, ARR is about $1.2M.
- Source: briefing pack §6, §10. ARR = adopting share × 9,800 accounts × $149 × 12. Survey: 610 of 9,800 admins replied (6%), averaging 9.4 seats vs 6.2 for the base. Paywall: 17 of 120 beta accounts bought after the free period.

=== Slide 5 ===
[Title] A flat $149 add-on attracts the heaviest users: paywall buyers cost $61 a month in inference, and the top tenth cost $131, leaving almost no margin
- PACKAGING: UNIT ECONOMICS
- Gross margin on a $149 add-on, by account usage (requests per month)
- What this means
[Chart: BAR_CLUSTERED]
  categories: Median beta account (1,900); Mean beta account (3,600); Paywall buyers (6,800); Top 10% of beta (14,500)
  series 's': 0.885235; 0.78255; 0.589262; 0.124161
- Usage is concentrated: the top 10% of beta accounts made 58% of all AI requests.
- Buyers self-select: paywall buyers made 6,800 requests a month, nearly 2× the beta mean and 3.6× the median.
- Flat price, variable cost: one price for 3 seats or 50 seats overcharges small shops and undercharges heavy users.
- Fix: price per seat and cap included usage, so revenue grows with account size and cost has a ceiling.
- Company floor is 75% gross margin. The accounts most likely to buy sit well below it. Revenue per account is fixed, while cost rises with use.
- Source: briefing pack §5–6. Inference cost = requests per month × $0.009 (contract fixed to Dec 2027). Gross margin = ($149 − inference) / $149, before the $0.6M a year of platform, evaluation and support cost.

=== Slide 6 ===
[Title] With a 600-request allowance, a per-seat Pro tier keeps gross margin above 75% in every case; uncapped heavy use would breach it
- PACKAGING: MARGIN GUARDRAIL
- Company gross margin, 33% of accounts on Pro
- Proposed Pro plan
[Table]
  | Scenario | Inference per Pro seat | Company GM | vs 75% floor |
  | Today (Core only) | — | 78.0% | ✓ Above |
  | Pro, beta-mean usage (444 requests per seat) | $4.00 | 76.5% | ✓ Above |
  | Pro, every seat uses the full allowance (600) | $5.40 | 75.9% | ✓ Above |
  | Pro, uncapped, every account at paywall-buyer usage (6,800 per account) | $9.87 | 74.1% | ✗ Breach |
- Price: $91 per seat per month (Core $79 + $12).
- Includes: Dispatch Copilot (schedule proposals, quote drafts, call summaries).
- Allowance: 600 requests per seat per month, pooled across the account. A 6.2-seat account gets 3,720; the median beta account used 1,900.
- Overage: $20 per 1,000 extra requests (55% margin at $0.009).
- Unit cost at beta-mean usage: $4.00 per seat per month, 67% margin on the uplift.
- Core: unchanged at $79; no Copilot.
- Floor: 75%. The allowance limits the worst case to $5.40 of inference per $12 seat uplift (55% margin on the uplift). Without it, heavy users push the company to 74.1%.
- Source: briefing pack §1, §5–6; appendix A1. Company GM = (ARR + Pro uplift − current COGS − inference − $0.6M platform) / (ARR + uplift). Scenario: 33% of accounts on Pro (2023 tier precedent) at 6.2 seats. Beta mean = 3,600 requests ÷ 8.1 seats ≈ 444 per seat.
