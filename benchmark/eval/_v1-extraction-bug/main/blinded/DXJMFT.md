## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Recommendation: do not launch Parcelbird Now in 2027; invest in the software business, which can reach profitability within current cash

**Executive summary:**
1. Market: The market is growing fast, but no operator makes money in it. US quick-commerce GMV grew 42% a year, 2022–25. Both incumbents lose money on every order (EBITDA margins −26% and −46%); QuickBasket closed 4 of its 11 cities.
2. Economics: A Parcelbird dark store would lose money on every order, and customers don't stay long enough to repay their acquisition cost. Loss of $6.17 per order at benchmark median, and $1.76 with best-observed inputs on every driver. Only the founders' plan (+$2.02) is profitable, and it beats the best site ever observed on all four drivers. Our pop-up lost $12.57 per order; 22% of its customers were still ordering in month 3.
3. Funding: Even on the founders' own numbers, the launch breaks the investors' cash constraints. The launch needs $9.5M; only $7.2M can be spent while keeping 12 months of runway. Adding today's core burn, cash runs out in month 13. At benchmark economics, 9 stores would lose $18M a year.
4. Alternative: The software business is a better use of the same cash. It breaks even in about month 26 at today's 60% growth (month 36 at 40%) and never drops below 12 months of runway, provided growth stays at 34% or more. Selling store-based fast delivery to pipeline grocers gives Parcelbird a share of the market's growth.
5. Decisions for the board: (1) decline the 2027 launch and do not extend the pop-up; (2) approve the $0.8M a year of software hires, phased against signed contracts; (3) build a store-based fast-delivery offer for the grocers in the pipeline; (4) adopt explicit re-entry triggers, tracked through our customers' sites, before quick commerce is reconsidered.
