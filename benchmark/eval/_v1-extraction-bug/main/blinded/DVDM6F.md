## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Launch Copilot in Q2 2027 inside a per-seat Pro plan: it is the only option that lifts NRR toward 105% and keeps gross margin above 75%

**Executive summary:**
1. Recommendation: launch Dispatch Copilot in Q2 2027 inside a new Pro plan at +$12 per seat per month ($91 vs $79 Core) with a pooled AI-usage allowance. Do not launch it as a $149 per-account add-on, and make the mobile app rewrite the next engineering priority. Plan on $1.7–2.9M of expansion ARR (+3.0–5.0 NRR points), not the $8M in the proposal.
2. Launch: a paid Copilot is needed to reach the NRR target, and the beta shows real value
3. Reaching 105% NRR takes +4 points, about $2.3M of net expansion a year. Removing every mobile-related churn would add only 3.8 points
4. In the beta, jobs per dispatcher-hour rose 11% against 2% for matched accounts. The gain is real, but volunteer bias inflates it
5. Routewise now includes AI in its Pro plan. AI explains at most ~$0.6M a year of lost new ARR, a third of Sales’ $1.9M estimate
6. Packaging and price: a per-seat Pro plan beats the $149 add-on, a free bundle and not launching
7. Purchases point to 7–14% buying the add-on, not the 46% who said they would: ~$1.2–2.5M ARR, mostly from the heaviest users
8. Pro at +$12 per seat costs every account 15% more. If 20–33% of accounts move to it: $1.7–2.9M ARR and +3.0–5.0 NRR points
9. Giving Copilot free in Core cuts company gross margin to 72–74%, below the 75% floor
10. Trade-offs: accept the two-quarter mobile delay on purpose, protect margin, and launch in stages
11. The mobile delay loses ~$0.3–0.8M ARR to churn that could have been avoided. Launching Copilot first still reaches 105% about two quarters sooner
12. An allowance of 500 requests per seat caps AI cost at $4.50 per $12 seat. Company gross margin stays at 76% or more
13. Test on non-volunteer accounts in Q1 and check results at end of Q3 (go-ahead if ≥13% have moved to Pro). The 14 Copilot engineers move to mobile in Q3 2027
