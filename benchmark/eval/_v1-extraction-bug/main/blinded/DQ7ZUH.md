## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Raise prices in 2027 where customers stay, hold them where customers are leaving

**Executive summary:**
1. Churn is rising almost only in the 2025 promotion cohort. Established SMB customers churn 2.1% a month (2.0% a year ago). The 2,100 promotion customers churn at an implied ~4.9% and make up ~56% of SMB churners. Mid-market (0.8%) and enterprise (0.2%) churn remains low.
2. The CFO is half right. Churn is not a broad price problem. But the promotion cohort is very price-sensitive: 52% of its churners cite price. And Inkwell’s $9 Starter plan has cut our SMB win rate from 51% to 38%.
3. A 12% rise on every plan is worth $7.6M gross in 2027, not $10.3M. 15% of mid-market ARR and 60% of enterprise ARR do not renew in 2027, and most enterprise contracts cap rises at 7%. In SMB, the rise nets anywhere from ~$1.2M to about zero once churn is counted.
4. Recommendation: raise prices segment by segment. +10% at mid-market renewal (the level tested in 2024). Enterprise: 7% where capped, 10% elsewhere. SMB list prices held in H1, with a Q1 test of +8% for established customers. Promotion-cohort prices frozen, plus an activation push. Est. $4.3M gross, $2.4–3.5M net.
5. The trade-off. On paper this is $0.3–1.8M less 2027 ARR than the CFO plan, but it avoids losing an extra ~320–640 SMB customers. NRR stays at ~112–113%. New customers still need to add ~$1.5–2.6M of ARR to reach +15%.
6. Decisions for December: approve the segmented plan, the SMB test and its go/no-go rule, and a single churn definition for board reporting.
