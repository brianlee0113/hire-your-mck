## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Margin is falling because of promotions, shrink and e-commerce delivery, not wages; fixing those first gets Harlow to ~3% by FY2028

**Executive summary:**
1. Operating margin fell from 3.4% to 1.0% in two years (FY2023–FY2025); operating income fell from $47.9M to $15.3M while sales grew 6%.
2. About 80% of the fall is gross margin (deeper promotions, more shrink) and e-commerce fulfilment. Store labor explains 13%: wages rose 9.4%, but 6% fewer hours per sales dollar offset most of it.
3. E-commerce loses ~$10M a year once third-party delivery and sales taken from our own stores are counted.
4. Replace the 8% across-the-board labor cut with six actions: cut loss-making promotions, restore fresh and receiving routines to cut shrink, reprice delivery, grow private label, exit the nine loss-making stores, and target labor savings where hours are heaviest.
5. Result: ~$39M operating income (2.8%) in FY2028 on cautious assumptions; $5.5M of named stretch levers take it to 3.1%. Management's draft plan reaches ~1.5%.
6. Start with promotions, delivery pricing and shrink: fast, low-capital, partly tested. They should lift FY2026 to ~$24–28M, above the $20M covenant.
7. Approve the plan in place of the draft labor-cut plan, and launch wave 1 now.
8. Approve in principle the exit of the three loss-making stores whose leases run beyond 2027 ($7.5M one-time); store-by-store cases come to the next board meeting.
9. Keep home delivery, but repriced, with delivered-order growth on hold until each order covers its cost.
