=== Slide 2 ===
[Title] Yes, raise prices in 2027, but not 12% on every plan: price mid-market and enterprise now, and hold SMB until the evidence is in
- EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- Churn is a cohort problem, not a market-wide price problem
- SMB monthly churn rose 2.0% → 3.1%; ~90% of the rise comes from the 2,100 customers who joined on the 2025 50%-off promotion. Other SMB customers churn at 2.1% (2.0% a year ago) and only 18% of them cite price (19% in 2025). Mid-market 0.6% → 0.8%; enterprise flat at 0.2%.
- The headroom is in mid-market and enterprise
- 76% of ARR, net revenue retention (NRR) of 112% and 118%; customers stay for integrations and the audit trail; a +10% price test showed no significant conversion loss. SMB is where Inkwell's $9 plan is winning: win rate 51% → 38%.
- The CFO's $10.3M is not available
- Only $7.6M can be repriced in 2027 (15% of mid-market and 60% of enterprise ARR do not renew; enterprise caps at 7%). After the likely churn response it is $4.5–6.3M.
- Recommended plan
- Mid-market +10% at renewal; enterprise at contract terms (7% where capped, 10% otherwise): +$4.3M. SMB list price held through June while a controlled +8% test runs; go or no-go for the second half (+$0.4M). Activation programme for the promotion cohort (+$0.3M). ≈ $98.9M exit ARR, exactly the +15% target, before any new-customer ARR; NRR ≈ 115%.
- The trade-off
- ≈ $1.6M less 2027 ARR than the CFO plan's base case on paper. In exchange: no wider price gap against Inkwell where Palisade is losing deals, and a measured answer on SMB price sensitivity before 5,900 customers are committed to it.
- Decisions needed in December: approve mid-market and enterprise renewal pricing · hold SMB and approve the test and June gate · fund the activation programme · restate the board churn metric
- Source: Palisade Docs briefing pack; team analysis. Calculations and assumptions are in the appendix.

=== Slide 16 ===
[Title] Appendix: how the numbers were built
- APPENDIX A · CALCULATIONS AND ASSUMPTIONS
- Baseline (no price change). Each segment's ARR × its trailing-12-month NRR: SMB 21.0 × 94% = 19.7; mid-market 41.0 × 112% = 45.9; enterprise 24.0 × 118% = 28.3; total $94.0M (+9.3%). Assumes 2027 repeats the trailing year and that the trailing NRR contains no price rise.
- Target. $86.0M × 1.15 = $98.9M exit-2027 ARR. Price effects are measured on exit ARR: every 2027 renewal counts in full.
- Repriceable ARR at +12%. SMB 21.0 × 12% = 2.52; mid-market 41.0 × 85% × 12% = 4.18; enterprise 9.6 renewing, 60% capped at 7% and 40% at 12% = 0.86; total $7.57M. If no renewing contract is capped: 1.15; if all are: 0.67.
- SMB churn response. 2023: +8% → +0.5 pts a month for six months, then +0.1 pts. Scaled linearly to 12%: 5.4% extra churn × $23.5M = $1.27M. Downside doubles it ($2.54M) for Inkwell.
- Mid-market response (downside only). +0.2 pts a month for six months on renewing ARR: $0.46M at 10%, $0.56M at 12%.
- Recommended price ARR. Mid-market 34.85 × 10% = 3.49; enterprise 5.76 × 7% + 3.84 × 10% = 0.79; total $4.27M.
- SMB +8% from July. Half of the core SMB ARR ($16.6M) reprices by year-end: 8.3 × 8% = 0.66, less the 2023-style response of 3% × 8.96 = 0.27; net ≈ $0.40M.
- Promotion cohort. Implied churn (5,900 × 3.1% − 3,800 × 2.1%) ÷ 2,100 = 4.9%. Activation split: with low senders churning 3× as often, core 2.1% implies 1.35% for activated customers; the cohort's 41% activation implies 2.9%; the rest (2.0 pts) is the discount ending and other factors.
- Activation value. 12-month loss on $4.4M at 4.9% a month = 45% ($2.0M); at 4.1% = 39% ($1.7M); saves ≈ $0.3M. At 2.1% = 22% ($1.0M); saves ≈ $1.0M (upside).
- Rounding. Figures are rounded to $0.1M; bars may not sum exactly.
- Source: Palisade Docs briefing pack; team analysis.
