## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Fix promotions, shrink and delivery pricing before cutting hours; with store exits, margin reaches 2.5% by FY2028, 3.0% with targeted hours

**Executive summary:**
1. Margin fell 2.35 percentage points in two years, mostly in gross margin and e-commerce, not wages
2. – Gross margin and e-commerce explain 79% of it; labor 13%
3. – Promotions and shrink cost $18M of FY2025 gross profit
4. Promotions and shrink are the biggest fix: a promotion reset adds $9.9M a year; hour cuts raise shrink
5. – Promotions hit 29% of sales, yet 41% call Harlow expensive
6. – ValuMart's pressure is local; chain-wide promotions do not pay
7. E-commerce and nine weak stores lose $13.5M a year; delivery pricing and store exits recover $9.1M
8. – Counted in full, e-commerce loses $10.4M a year
9. – The tested delivery fee cuts that loss by $2.5M
10. No-capital moves first protect the covenant; the plan reaches 2.5% by FY2028, 3.0% with targeted hours
11. – The 2027 wage rise cuts FY2028 margin from 3.0% to 2.5%
12. – Management's hours cut leaves FY2028 margin at 1.0–1.5%

**Recommendation:** Fix promotions, shrink and delivery pricing before cutting hours. Start the three no-capital moves at once and report the covenant forecast to the lender monthly; exit the nine loss-making stores (six as leases end, three after board vote); keep a targeted hours cut as the lever that closes the gap to 3.0%, not as the plan's first move. Ask the board now for the plan and the three store-exit votes; targeted hours wait for the FY2027 gate.

**Alternatives considered:**
- Management's 8% hours cut (draft plan): Rejected as the first move: protects the covenant (FY2026 operating income $22–29.5M) but shrink and the wage rise would erase its saving by FY2028 (net −$0.02M); FY2028 margin would sit at 1.0–1.5%; it leaves the real causes in place. Next steps: Stop the 8% hours cut.
- Targeted hours cut of about 4%, only from the most-staffed stores after shrink is fixed: Chosen as a later lever: 'the better use of the idea'; closes the gap to 3.0%, subject to the FY2027 gate.
- Network-wide (chain-wide) promotions to defend against ValuMart: Rejected: network-wide promotions do not pay back; price investment belongs in ValuMart's trade areas, funded from the promotion savings.
- Freezing e-commerce at today's size (part of management's draft plan): Rejected implicitly: the freeze keeps the e-commerce loss of about $10.4M a year. Deck instead recommends rolling out the tested delivery fee, which keeps delivery (owners want delivery kept in some form).

- The pack gives neither the deep-promotion stores' promo share nor how many stores sit near a ValuMart
- Shrink gain: the pack does not give each quartile's sales, so the gain assumes equal sales; theft (35% of shrink) is not addressed
- Delivery fee: the test is small (4-store, 10-week) and the result is assumed to hold network-wide; picking stays at $7.40 and carrier at $13.80; treating every lost sale as new overstates the cost
- Store exits: per-store results are not in the briefing pack; closure costs for the six lapsing leases are not given and would also count against the $25M limit; exits remove $88.5M of sales, lowering the sales base for the 3.0% target
