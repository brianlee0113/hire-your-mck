## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Do not launch Parcelbird Now in 2027; sell fast delivery to grocers as software instead

**Executive summary:**
1. Quick commerce is growing, but shoppers are not loyal enough to fill a store affordably – US quick-commerce sales grew 42% in 2025, to $5.1B – Filling nine stores would take about $27M of paid acquisition (p. 5–8)
2. No observed dark store makes money per order; only the founders' plan does – At benchmark medians a store loses about $6 on every order – Our own pop-up lost about $12.60 an order (p. 10–17)
3. The launch breaks the 12-month runway rule even if every plan assumption holds – The plan needs $9.5M; only $7.2M sits above the runway floor (p. 19–22)
4. Software is the better use of the cash, and its grocers want fast delivery – The grocer pipeline is worth 87% of today's ARR, for $0.8M a year of hires (p. 24–27)
5. Decline the launch, build fast delivery for grocers, reopen only if four signals turn – Grocer software alone meets both investor tests, and goes live in Q3 2027 (p. 29–33)
