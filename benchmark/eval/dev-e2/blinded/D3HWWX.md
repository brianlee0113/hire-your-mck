**Recommendation:** Fix promotions, delivery fees and weak stores now and cut hours last: promotions and delivery fees go first, as they alone clear the FY2026 covenant; exit the nine loss-making stores (board to vote on the three early exits, $7.5M one-time), grow private label, restore shrink controls, and cut labor hours 8% last, after the shrink gate; decide on a second promotion wave to about 21% of sales if sales near ValuMart hold, to reach 3.0%.

**Alternatives considered:**
- Management's 8% store-hours cut as the first move (labor plan): Rejected as first move: net of shrink and the 2027 wage rise it leaves FY2028 operating income near today's, at $16M (1.0% of sales); "targets the smallest cause of the decline and gives back almost half its saving in shrink". Kept as the last step, after the shrink gate.
- Keeping the three long-lease stores (no early exits): Would cost the plan $2.2M a year; a payback of about 3.4 years justifies the vote.
- Deeper promotions as the value signal versus growing private label: Private label is a cheaper value signal than deeper promotions; each share point earns 12 pp more, where promotion costs 14.
- Freezing e-commerce: A freeze limits the damage but does not end it.
- Second, deeper promotion wave to about 21% of sales: Needed to reach 3.0%; decided in FY2027 H1, starts only if sales near ValuMart hold.
- Bringing part of the hours cut forward in the best-staffed stores: Fallback if promotional share is above 26.0% from FY2026 Q2.
- Return to the $75 free-delivery threshold: Fallback if online sales fall more than about 12% after the fee change.
