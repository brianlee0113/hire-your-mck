- Raising price for the promotion cohort: Rejected: the promotion cohort keeps its price; 52% of its leavers cite price, and most left within two months of the last price step; activation, not price, is this cohort's lever.

- Exit survey covers churned customers who answered it; "price" = main reason "price / found a cheaper option"
- Assumes the 60% of enterprise ARR under 7% caps is spread evenly across renewal years; $7.6M is before any churn response to the rise
- Net revenue retention as reported in the briefing pack (definition not given)
- Existing customers at renewal were not tested, so the first 2027 renewals are the real test; 12% is above the tested 10%
- Breakeven simplifications: lost ARR ≈ extra monthly churn × 12 months × renewing ARR at the new price (simple, not compounded); customers who leave over price are of average size; expansion stays at today's rate, only churn reacts; mid-market churn starts from its H1 2026 level of 0.8%
- SMB rise: the extra churn is applied to all of their ARR at the new $16.20 price for twelve months; based on the 2023 reaction
- Growth = trailing NRR by segment plus price uplift, net of the SMB churn response, before new customers. CFO row: 2023 reaction scaled by 12 ÷ 8 on all SMB ARR, generous for the promotion cohort
- Enterprise at 7% on capped and 12% on other ARR renewing in 2027 in every row
- The onboarding programme's cost is not in the briefing pack and is not netted out
- Whether the cohort can be activated at all is one of the pack's open questions; the gates track it
- SMB's 94% NRR may include the cohort's step from 50% off to full price; the pack does not define NRR, so stress removes up to $2.2M
- Stress case: mid-market churn up by half; SMB NRR without the promotion step-up; stress gap about $3.3M for new business to cover
