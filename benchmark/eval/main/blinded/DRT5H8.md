## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Executive summary: raise prices in 2027, but target mid-market and enterprise and keep SMB prices flat until a test proves they can rise

**Executive summary:**
1. Answer: yes, with a targeted increase. At 2027 renewals, raise mid-market +12% and enterprise up to its contract terms. Hold SMB prices, exempt the 2025 promotion cohort and test core SMB before raising it. The plan adds ≈$4.1M of net ARR (the CFO's $10.3M headline is ≈$5.0M once timing and churn are counted), keeps NRR above 100%, and leaves the 15% growth target needing only ≈$0.9M of new-customer ARR.
2. 1. Churn is rising because of one cohort, not because customers are reacting to price. About 90% of the SMB churn rise (2.0% → 3.1% a month) comes from the 2,100 customers who joined on the 2025 half-price promotion. They churn at ≈4.9% a month. Churn among other SMB customers only moved from 2.0% to 2.1%, and the share of them citing price held at 18% (19% in 2025).
3. 2. Pricing power sits in mid-market and enterprise, not SMB. Mid-market and enterprise are 76% of ARR, with 112–118% NRR and monthly churn of 0.8% or less. A +10% mid-market test showed no significant conversion loss, and Inkwell has not appeared in deals above 200 seats. In SMB, Inkwell sells a $9 plan and Palisade's win rate against it fell from 51% to 38%.
4. 3. The CFO's $10.3M is really ≈$5.0M. The targeted plan keeps ≈$4.1M of that with less risk. Renewal timing and the 7% enterprise caps leave $7.6M that can be repriced in 2027, and expected churn cuts that to ≈$5.0M. Leaving SMB out costs ≈$1.0M in the base case. It raises the downside floor from $2.5M to $3.1M and avoids raising prices on customers who are already leaving over price.
5. 4. Roll it out in stages, with guardrails. Reprice mid-market and enterprise renewals from January, and drop mid-market to +10% if Q1 churn goes above 1.0% a month. Run a randomised +8% test on core SMB in Q1 and extend it from May only if it passes. Exempt the promotion cohort and fund its onboarding. Reset the budget line and define the churn figure the board sees.

=== Slide 13 ===
[Title] A three-month randomised test will show whether core SMB customers can bear +8% before Palisade commits to it

- Test design
- Who: core SMB customers renewing in Q1 2027. The promotion cohort is excluded.
- Split: 50/50 at random by account. Treatment +8% at renewal with standard notice; control at today's price.
- Main metric: monthly logo churn in the 90 days after renewal, treatment minus control.
- Secondary metrics: downgrades to cheaper plans, share of exit surveys citing price, Inkwell mentions.
- Statistical power: with ≈1,900 customers per arm and ≈6.3% three-month churn in the control group, the standard error of the difference is ≈0.8pp. That separates a 2023-type reaction (+1.5pp over 3 months) from break-even (+3.7pp) by ≈2.6 standard errors.
- Caveat: annual-plan customers renew only once a year. If they are a large share of SMB, fewer accounts will renew in Q1, so extend the test to six months.

=== Slide 14 ===
[Title] Onboarding is worth funding, but it won't halve SMB churn in two quarters; plan on a 15–20% cut for new customers

- SMB monthly logo churn: today and with onboarding (%)
- Why the 'halve churn' claim doesn't hold
- Halving means 3.1% → 1.55%. Even if every SMB customer behaved like an activated one, churn would be ≈1.35%, so halving would need almost every customer activated.
- First-month activation can't be applied retroactively to the 5,900 existing customers. Onboarding mainly affects new sign-ups.
- A realistic aim is to raise new-customer activation from 72% to 85%. That cuts new-cohort churn from 2.1% to ≈1.75% (−17%).
- Blended SMB churn will fall anyway as the promotion cohort shrinks. Measure onboarding by cohort, or its effect will look bigger than it is.
- Implication
- Fund onboarding for new customers and outreach to the promotion cohort as the churn levers. Keep them out of the 2027 ARR plan until cohort data shows the effect.
