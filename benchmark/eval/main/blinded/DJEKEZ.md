**Governing thought:** Recommendation: do not launch Parcelbird Now in 2027 — grow the software business that already sells into this market

**Executive summary:**
1. The market is growing, but no one in it makes money. US quick-commerce sales grew ~42% a year to $5.1B in 2025, yet both incumbents lose money on every order (Zipcart −$1.90, QuickBasket −$3.40), and QuickBasket closed 4 of its 11 cities.
2. Our own data say a Parcelbird dark store would lose money. At the median of the 14 sites that run our software, a store loses $6.17 per order after site costs. Our Columbus pop-up lost $12.57.
3. The plan’s +$2.02 per order needs every input to beat the best site on record: average order $45 vs $42, 3.5 vs 3.0 orders per rider-hour, 1,800 vs 1,400 orders a day. Miss the order-value or rider-productivity assumption alone and the store loses money.
4. It fails both of the board’s conditions. Even on plan, $2.5M would be left at month 18, about 6 months of runway against a 12-month floor. At benchmark economics, nine stores would lose ~$18M a year, 1.5× our cash.
5. Selling to grocers is the funded way into quick commerce. 9 late-stage deals are worth $0.86M of ARR, and grocers in the pipeline want fast delivery from their own stores. Scaling to win them costs ~$0.8M a year.
6. Decisions asked of the board: (1) decline the nine-store launch; (2) approve ~$0.8M a year for 2 engineers and 2 sales hires; (3) adopt the triggers on page 14 for reopening the operator question, with a review in H1 2028.

=== Slide 16 ===
[Title] Judgement calls made in preparing this paper (no clarification was available)
- APPENDIX B
- Definition of contribution. The pack does not define “contribution profit per order”. We show order margin (before store fixed costs) and contribution (after them). The plan is positive on both, so the conclusion does not depend on the choice.
- Reading of the $9.5M. We treat it generously, as all company cash needed to month 18. The stricter reading (Parcelbird Now only, with the existing $0.4M monthly burn on top) leaves −$4.7M.
- Runway. Cash ÷ monthly burn, holding today’s burn flat. This ignores 60% ARR growth, so it understates runway for the software option.
- Zipcart playbook test. We assume the $3.99 fee is all extra revenue (the pack does not say if it is net of payment cost), and hold rider productivity at the median despite 14% fewer orders. Both choices favour the plan.
- Pop-up growth path. The ~180 to ~410 orders a day (week 1 to week 6) assumes steady 18% weekly growth that sums to 11,800 orders. It is our estimate, not reported data.
- Waterfall order. The assumptions affect each other, so step sizes on page 7 depend on the order shown. The one-at-a-time results are shown alongside.
- One-store pilot. The month-8 runway breach assumes today’s $0.4M monthly burn plus a benchmark-median store (−$2.0M a year) and $0.6M capex.
- Channel conflict. The risk of competing with grocer customers is our inference; the pack does not say where pipeline grocers operate.
