## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Fix promotions, shrink and delivery pricing, not store hours, to get close to 3.0%

**Executive summary:**
1. Gross margin and e-commerce, not wages, explain nearly four-fifths of the fall in operating income
2. – Operating income fell from $47.9M to $15.3M while sales grew 6%
3. – 3.0% needs about $30M more a year; the FY2026 covenant needs $4.7M more
4. Fewer promotions, more private label and less shrink would win back about $17M of gross margin a year
5. – 38% of FY2025 promotional events lost money
6. – Shrink is 3.1% of sales in the least-staffed stores, 2.3% in the best
7. Store hours should be moved, not cut, and the weakest stores exited as their leases end
8. – An 8% hours cut could give back up to $10M of its $14.5M saving
9. – Exiting the nine loss-making stores adds about $6.6M a year
10. E-commerce loses about $10M a year; the tested delivery fee cuts that by $2.8M and keeps the service
11. – Each home delivery loses about $12, seven times a collected order
12. The plan gets to about 3.0% by FY2028 for $7.5M; its first two actions can clear the FY2026 covenant
13. – Started at once, promotions and the fee lift FY2026 to about $23M
14. – About $0.4M short of 3.0%; it also needs the 2027 wage rise absorbed
