## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Fix gross margin and delivery economics rather than cutting labor: four quick moves protect the FY2026 covenant, and the full plan restores 3.0% by FY2028

**Executive summary:**
1. The decline is mainly in gross margin and e-commerce, not wages. Operating margin fell 2.35 pts (3.37% → 1.02%). Gross margin lost 1.02 pts (promotions −0.70, shrink −0.50, private label +0.18) and e-commerce fulfillment 0.84 pts. Store labor cost only 0.30 pts, because 6% higher productivity absorbed most of the 9.4% wage rise.
2. Management's 8% labor cut would not reach the target. It saves $14.6M, but thinner staffing is linked to higher shrink worth an estimated $5–10M. FY2026 operating income would land at $19–24M against the $20M covenant, and FY2028 margin at only 0.9–1.2%.
3. A margin-led plan restores ~3.0% for ~$20M of one-time cost. Six levers backed by Harlow's own data (promotions, shrink, labor rebalancing, six lease lapses, private label, delivery fees) take operating income to ~$36M (2.5%) after the 2027 wage step. Three negotiated levers (vendor funding, three lease exits, the delivery contract) add ~$6M, for ~$42M, or 3.0%.
4. Act first on what is fast, cheap and proven, and hold contingencies. In the first 90 days: roll out the tested delivery fee, cancel money-losing promotions, protect fresh and receiving hours, and reject the 3% delivery-rate rise. FY2026 then reaches ~$24M (about $4M above the covenant). 3.0% has no cushion, so contingency levers are pre-agreed.
5. Decisions requested today: adopt this plan in place of the 8% labor cut; approve up to $20.5M of one-time spend; schedule a vote on exiting three long-lease stores.
