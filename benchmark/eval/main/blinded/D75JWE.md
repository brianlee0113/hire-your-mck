## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** do not launch Parcelbird Now in 2027. Scale the software business, which reaches profitability within current cash, and sell fast delivery to grocers

**Executive summary:**
1. Answer: No. As an operator, Parcelbird would lose money on every order at any level of performance observed so far, and the plan would break the board's cash rules within a year. The software business has a funded path to profit and still captures the growth in quick commerce.
2. A. The economics fail: the market grows but makes no profit, and a Parcelbird order would lose money
3. The market grew 41% a year to $5.1B (2022–25), but both incumbents lose money on each order; QuickBasket closed 4 of its 11 cities
4. At the median of Parcelbird's 14 customer sites, an order loses $6.17 after site costs. The best composite still loses $1.76, and the pop-up lost $12.57
5. The plan's +$2.02 needs every driver to beat the best site ever observed. At the standard 6% promotions it falls to +$0.22
6. With $38 CAC and 22% of customers left at month 3, acquisition cost is never earned back at benchmark economics
7. B. Parcelbird cannot afford to find out: every dark-store option breaks the 12-month runway floor
8. If the $9.5M plan runs on top of today's software burn, runway falls below 12 months in month 2 and cash runs out in month 15
9. At benchmark economics, nine stores need about $37M in their first full year (operating loss, CAC, capex) against $12M of cash, and no new round is expected
10. Even a one-store pilot (about $3.8M) keeps runway below 12 months from month 5 to month 18
11. C. Scaling the software business is the better route into the same market
12. The pipeline holds $3.55M of ARR, 1.4× what 60% growth needs in 2027. The $0.8M/yr of hires pay back with about 11 contracts
13. Base case: cash break-even in about month 26, cash never below $5.4M, runway never below 24 months. Holds at 45% growth
14. Pipeline grocers want fast delivery from their own stores. Selling that capability gives Parcelbird the growth and leaves the operating risk with grocers
15. Decisions requested: (1) do not launch in 2027; (2) approve $0.8M/yr for 2 engineers and 2 sales hires, focused on a store-based fast-delivery module; (3) set objective triggers for reopening the question (slide 18).
