## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Do not launch Parcelbird Now in 2027; put the cash into the software business, which can break even without new funding

**Executive summary:**
1. The market grows about 40% a year, but no operator has yet shown it can make money in it – Zipcart loses $1.90 and QuickBasket $3.40 on each order (p. 5–8)
2. At benchmark inputs an order loses about $6.2 after site costs; the plan needs every driver above the best site – Break-even would need a $63 basket, against the $34 median (p. 10–17)
3. Few customers keep ordering, so the $38 it costs to win each one is slow or impossible to recover – Only 22% of pop-up customers still ordered in month three (p. 19–22)
4. The launch leaves about 6 months of runway even if all goes to plan; the software can break even on current cash – The software breaks even in early 2029 with about $5.4M left (p. 24–29)
5. Decline the launch; put $0.8M a year into the software and store-based delivery for grocers, with gates to reopen – Reopen only when a customer site makes money after site costs (p. 31–34)
