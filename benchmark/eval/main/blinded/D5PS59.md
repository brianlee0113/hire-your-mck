## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Launch Copilot in Q2 2027 as part of a new Pro tier at +$12 per seat. A $149 add-on would earn less, and Copilot free in Core would breach the margin floor

**Executive summary:**
1. Launch. The competitive threat is real, though smaller than claimed
2. Routewise put AI scheduling in its Pro plan, and since then our win rate in deals against them has fallen from 44% to 37%. At most that is ~110 deals, or $0.6M of new ARR a year, not the $1.9M the Sales memo implies.
3. In the beta, jobs per dispatcher-hour rose 11%, against 2% for matched accounts. The beta accounts volunteered, so this is an upper bound.
4. Package it as a Pro tier. The $8M add-on case rests on the survey
5. When beta users actually hit a $149 paywall, 14% bought, against 46% in the survey. That means at most $2.5M of add-on ARR.
6. A flat $149 attracts heavy users: their inference costs eat 41% of the price, and 88% for the top accounts. A per-seat Pro tier grows with account size and matches Routewise. Base case: ~$2.2M ARR, +3.8 NRR points and a 76.6% company gross margin.
7. Including Copilot free in Core would take gross margin to 72–74%, below the 75% floor.
8. Guardrails. Protect mobile and test before we scale
9. Launching first delays the mobile rewrite by two quarters, which costs ~$0.5M of churn we could have avoided. Holding Copilot back until mobile ships would forgo ~$1.1M or more of Copilot ARR.
10. Go or no-go in March: if a random-sample Pro offer points to adoption below 15%, the 14 engineers go back to mobile.
11. Decisions requested today: 1. Approve a Q2 2027 launch of Pro: $91 per seat ($79 Core + $12), including Copilot with 600 AI requests per seat per month, pooled per account. 2. Reject the $149 add-on and the free-in-Core option. 3. Assign 14 engineers to Copilot for Q1–Q2, and re-baseline the mobile rewrite two quarters later with a fixed date. 4. Approve the Q1 price test and the March go/no-go checkpoint.
