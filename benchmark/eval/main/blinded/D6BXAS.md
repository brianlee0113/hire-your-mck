## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Fix promotions, shrink and delivery pricing first. Wages are not the main problem, and the 3% target is reachable

**Executive summary:**
1. What happened: Operating margin fell from 3.37% to 1.02% in two years (operating income $47.9M → $15.3M) even though sales grew 6%. Wages explain only 0.30 of the 2.35-point fall. Gross margin (−1.02 pts: deeper promotions and higher shrink) and e-commerce fulfillment (−0.84 pts) explain ~80%.
2. Why the draft plan falls short: The 8% labor-hour cut and the e-commerce freeze cannot reach 3%: even if the full $14.5M sticks, margin reaches ~2.0%. If shrink responds as it did in FY2023–25, up to ~$10M leaks back, and FY2026 operating income lands at ~$20M, on the covenant line.
3. What we recommend: Seven levers worth ~$30M a year (promotion reset, vendor funding, targeted labor, shrink, six store exits at lease end, private label, delivery pricing) take margin to ~3.2% at FY2025 scale for ≤$17.5M one-time cost (cap $25M). Delivery is kept but priced closer to its cost.
4. What to do first: In the next 90 days: roll out the tested delivery fee chain-wide, drop money-losing promotions and reopen vendor funding, restore receiving and produce checks in low-labor stores, and serve notice on the six stores whose leases end by 2027. FY2026 operating income: ~$27M vs the $20M covenant.
5. Decisions today: Approve the plan in place of the across-the-board labor cut; approve the delivery-fee change. The other three loss-making stores return to the board for a vote in FY2027.
