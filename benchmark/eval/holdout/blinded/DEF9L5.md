## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Keep connector C: redesign it now, fix bundle service, and decide any 2029 sunset on evidence

**Executive summary:**
1. C's 8% margin comes from the cost allocation; C is actually the portfolio's highest-contribution connector
2. C earns $3.8M direct contribution, a 63% contribution margin vs 50% for D, 50% for A and 42% for B
3. Only $0.4M of C's $3.3M allocated cost is avoidable; the other $2.9M would move onto A, B and D
4. Exiting C in 2027 loses $4.75M in the first year and $2.65M every year after, and breaks two non-negotiables
5. Even if D wins the optimistic 25% of C revenue, D adds only $0.75M; break-even would need $6.8M of D sales (113% of C)
6. The $2.1M penalty alone is 2.3x the $0.9M cash envelope; C sits in 9 of 14 major bundles with no validated substitute
7. The customer case for exit does not hold up: the real problem is bundle fill, caused by A and B
8. The 9-point renewal gap is confounded by hospital size, legacy equipment and customer-success coverage; there is no before/after test
9. C's 98% on-time figure leaves out bundle misses caused by A and B. Bundle fill is 91% vs the 95% contracted, and exiting C would not fix it
10. Recommended: redesign C now (+$2.4M over 2027–29), recover bundle fill, then hand the team to D
11. The $0.55M redesign pays back in ~5 months of savings and still breaks even at 19% of the $1.25M estimate; it fits within the $0.9M
12. Renew the two bundles without exit notice and with a consent-based D path; hold a Q4 2028 gate on a 2029 sunset
