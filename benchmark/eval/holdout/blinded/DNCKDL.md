**Governing thought:** Don't give exclusivity away before it is earned: sign a nonexclusive OEM pilot now and make North American exclusivity conditional on certification and audited volumes

**Executive summary:**
1. Recommendation: Decline Option X as drafted. Sign Option N, a 12-month nonexclusive pilot at $350 per robot, and agree up front the gates under which Option M (limited exclusivity) takes effect: certification passed, two audited quarters at about 9,000 robots a year, and contract protections signed.
2. 1 The deal is worth less than it is being presented as
3. The $2.0M is a recoupable advance, not extra revenue: three-year OEM cash is $8.7M, not $10.7M. After $80/robot support, $0.9M/yr engineering, the $0.45M assessment and displaced direct sales, exclusivity is worth +$2.2M to +$2.8M at plan but only −$0.04M to +$0.55M at 65% volumes.
4. 2 The evidence the board requires for irreversible exclusivity does not exist yet
5. Lab uptime is 98.8% against a 99.5% production bar. The third-party assessment has not started. OEM volumes are unaudited, and the 14,000 'booked devices' are a forecast. The growth claim rests on correlation. The draft lacks audit rights, a royalty floor, release triggers and code escrow.
6. 3 The phased N→M structure beats every alternative
7. It earns $50 more per robot, keeps the direct channel (~$1.48M of gross margin on new ARR in 2028), and matches X's value even if the OEM ships 24–32% fewer robots. It holds cash at least $0.55M above the $2.6M buffer in the worst case, and it fails none of the board's conditions.
8. 4 Gates, signposts and next steps make the decision reversible
9. Start the $0.45M assessment now; it is on the critical path for every OEM route. Convert to exclusivity only after certification and audited shipments of at least 2,250 robots a quarter. If the OEM insists on exclusivity at signing, walk away and fall back to direct sales plus the integration-firm pilot.
