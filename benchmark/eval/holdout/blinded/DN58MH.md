## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Retain and optimize East through its 2029 lease end and test the cross-dock: closing saves at most $0.10M a year, not $1.3M, and fails or cannot yet prove the board's tests

**Executive summary:**
1. Closing East does not pay once routing and handling are counted
2. Serving East from Central nets $0.10M a year, not the $1.3M claimed, and zero at 2028 volume, before the $0.75M exit fee
3. The cross-dock costs $0.15M a year more than today: $0.60M of extra transport outweighs $0.45M of fixed cost removed
4. Optimized retention (K) is cheapest in the base case: $8.44M in 2027, $0.20–0.45M a year below either closure
5. Closure also breaks, or cannot yet prove, the board's binding constraints
6. Close to Central: 90.3% against the 92% contract; Central at 100% in 2027 and 108% in 2028; $1.85M one-time with its unbudgeted retrofit, against $0.9M
7. Cross-dock: 93.9% on paper, but from a six-week, non-holiday pilot with no all-ZIP definition and no promotion guarantee
8. Retain and optimize: 95.1% network rate, Central at 47% of capacity in 2028, and $0.15M one-time
9. Optimize now, test the cross-dock, and decide the 2029 lease at a Q3 2028 gate
10. Approve Option K and a cross-dock test capped at $0.25M through holiday 2027 and spring 2028, measured on all ZIP codes
11. Waiting avoids the $0.75M exit fee and settles whether the $0.85M minimum is extra or credited, a swing of $0.85M a year
12. Drop close-to-Central: its $5-per-order penalty makes it no cheaper than today at 2028 volume, even if Central were expanded
