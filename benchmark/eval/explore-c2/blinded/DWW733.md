## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Harlow can lift operating margin to about 3.2% by FY2028 by fixing promotions, shrink and delivery pricing instead of cutting store hours across the board

**Executive summary:**
1. Wages are not the main cause: promotions, shrink and e-commerce explain 87% of the margin fall since FY2023 – Operating margin fell from 3.37% to 1.02% in two years; store labor explains only 13% of it (p. 4, 5)
2. An 8% cut in store hours would miss the target and put fresh produce at risk – A shrink rise like that of FY2023–25 would erase about half of its $14.5M saving (p. 7, 8)
3. Promotions are the largest lever, worth 0.7 percentage points of margin – Returning to 24% of sales needs cuts only among the 38% of events that lose money (p. 10–12)
4. Home delivery lost about $10M in FY2025; the tested pricing keeps the service and saves about $2.5M a year – The team's 6.1% margin leaves out delivery costs and counts every online sale as new (p. 14–16)
5. Five moves reach about 3.2% by FY2028, and the three that need no capital come first to clear the covenant – The 2027 minimum-wage rise, up to $7.3M a year, is the main risk to the plan (p. 18–21)

**Recommendation:** Recommended: five moves — stop money-losing promotional events and return promotions to 24% of sales; roll out the tested delivery pricing ($7.99 fee, free above $100); replace the 8% across-the-board hour cut with fixed fresh and receiving routines in the leanest half and 8% fewer hours in the best-staffed quarter; exit the nine loss-making stores (six at lease expiry, three after a board vote); and grow private label at its FY2023–25 pace — lifting operating margin to about 3.2% by FY2028. Promotions, delivery pricing and store hours go first because they need no capital and can clear the FY2026 covenant.

**Alternatives considered:**
- Management draft: 8% across-the-board cut in store hours, e-commerce frozen: Rejected: would reach only about 2.0% operating margin even with the full $14.5M saving, short of the 3.0% target; a shrink rise like FY2023–25 would erase about half the saving ($7M net); cuts hours in the leanest stores where shrink is already 3.1%; delivery still loses about $10M a year. Can meet the FY2026 covenant if the full saving lands in FY2026.
- Category team's case for deep promotions: Its evidence (comparable sales of +1.8% in the deepest-promotion stores, against +0.2%) measures sales, not profit.
- Cutting promotions that defend traffic near ValuMart: Not cut; reviewed event by event rather than cut.
- Keeping current delivery pricing (free above $75): Free delivery on most baskets makes each delivered order a loss; replaced by tested pricing that keeps the service and saves about $2.5M a year.

**Assumptions and uncertainties:**
- The 2027 minimum-wage rise, up to $7.3M a year, is the main risk to the plan; not included in the 3.2% estimate and would leave margin near 2.7%
- ValuMart's response to fewer promotions is a risk
- Sales of $1,412M: FY2025's $1,503M less sales lost from store exits and the delivery test, with no market growth
- The only known one-time cost is the $7.5M of lease exits
- Private label keeps gaining 0.75 percentage points of sales a year at its 12 percentage-point margin premium
- Sales moved off promotion stay with Harlow at regular price, and the margin gap of 14 percentage points holds
- Not counted: recovering vendor funding from 46% toward the FY2023 level of 55% of markdowns
- Shrink rising another 0.5 percentage points of FY2025 sales under the 8% hour cut, as it did when hours per $1,000 of sales fell 6%; lost sales from checkout waits and stock-outs not counted
- Only 30% of online sales are new to Harlow (customer survey of 2,300); contribution of 20 cents per new dollar is finance's estimate
- Delivery roll-out assumes none of the 2% lost online sales return to stores; provider proposed a 3% rate rise at 2026 renewal, about $0.3M a year
- 25% of the nine exited stores' $118.0M sales moves to nearby stores at 12% contribution; four-wall EBITDA excludes depreciation and overhead, so gain is if anything understated
- FY2026 covenant is met if the first three moves deliver a quarter of their $19.2M run-rate
- Timing follows the briefing pack, which treats FY2026 as the first plan year
- Margins and gains are team estimates; data are synthetic benchmark data

**Next steps:**
