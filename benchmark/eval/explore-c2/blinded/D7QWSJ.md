**Executive summary:**
1. Gross margin and e-commerce, not wages, cut the margin by 2.35 percentage points in two years
2. Store labor explains only 13%: fewer hours offset about 70% of the wage rise
3. Management's 8% hour cut targets the smallest driver and could give much of its saving back as shrink
4. Shrink is 3.1% of sales in least-staffed stores, against 2.3%
5. Up to $10M of the $14.5M saving could return as shrink
6. Promotions are the largest lever: returning to their FY2023 share would add about $9.9M a year
7. Promotions reached 29% of sales; 41% now call Harlow expensive
8. Shoppers come for fresh produce (58%) and convenience (44%)
9. Repricing delivery and exiting nine loss-making stores would add about $9.5M a year and keep the service
10. E-commerce loses about $10.4M a year; the tested fee cuts $2.8M
11. Exiting nine stores adds about $6.6M; six leases end by 2027
12. The actions reach about 3.0% by FY2028 for $7.5M of one-time cost; delivery and promotions come first
13. FY2026 income would reach about $25M, above the $20M covenant
14. The 2027 wage rise, up to $7.3M a year, is the main risk

**Recommendation:** Fix promotions, shrink, delivery and weak stores, not store hours, to reach 3.0% by FY2028: approve the margin plan and vote on exiting the three stores whose leases run beyond 2027 ($7.5M); roll out the tested delivery fee; cut the money-losing promotional events; withdraw the 8% hour cut and restore produce rotation and receiving checks; exit nine loss-making stores; keep the private-label pace. Delivery repricing and promotion cuts come first.

**Alternatives considered:**
- Management's draft plan: 8% cut in scheduled store labor hours: Rejected: targets the smallest driver (store labor, 13% of decline) and could give back up to $10M of its $14.5M saving through higher shrink; even with no shrink effect lifts FY2028 margin only to about 2.0%, short of the 3.0% target; FY2026 income $29.8M on paper but about $19.8M if shrink rises as in FY2023–25 (covenant headroom may vanish); fewer hours where shrink is already highest. Next step: withdraw the 8% hour cut.
- Recommended plan (five actions: promotions, store exits, shrink, private label, delivery fee): Chosen: about 3.0% FY2028 margin, about $25M FY2026 income above $20M covenant, $7.5M one-time cost within the $25M budget, keeps delivery and the fresh offer.
- Matching a discounter's (ValuMart's) promotional depth: Rejected: 'Defend traffic with fresh produce and convenience, not by matching a discounter's promotional depth'; heavier promotions have not bought a cheaper image.
- Delivery frozen at today's size (management's draft) vs. kept and repriced at tested fees: Repricing chosen: management's draft leaves delivery losing about $10.4M a year; the tested fee ($7.99, free above $100) cuts the loss by about $2.8M and keeps home delivery, respecting the owners' wish to keep delivery.

- Fictional company; synthetic benchmark data from the briefing pack
- Steps are computed from the income statement and may not add to the total because of rounding
- Hour-cut upper case assumes shrink tracks hours as it did in FY2023–25: all of the FY2023–25 rise in shrink (2.1% to 2.6%) is attributed to the 6.0% fall in hours per $1,000 of sales; shrink responds in proportion to hours (8% cut adds about 0.67 pp)
- If only waste and receiving errors (65% of shrink) respond to hours, the shrink loss is about $6.5M
- Effects on checkout waits and stock-outs are not counted
- Theft, the remaining 35% of shrink, is less tied to staffing
- FY2028 sales ($1,412M) assume the store exits and delivery fee change
- Promotion gain: sales leaving promotion are bought at regular price, earning the 14 pp of margin promotions give up; vendor funding recovery not counted (upside)
- Stores within three miles of a ValuMart keep today's promotional depth until weekly comparable sales show the cuts elsewhere held traffic; traffic loss near ValuMart is not sized
- E-commerce: only 30% of online sales are new to Harlow (survey, n = 2,300); contribution of 20 cents per new dollar is a finance estimate including packaging and allocated store costs
- Delivery fee rollout: carrier and picking costs per order held at FY2025 levels; based on a 4-store, 10-week test
- Store exits: 25% of sales transfer to nearby Harlow stores at 12% contribution, as in past closures; $1.4M less gain per 10 pp less transfer
- Phasing is a team assumption; FY2026 is treated as the first plan year, as in management's draft; FY2026 figures are team estimates
- State minimum wage +4% in 2027 is the main risk, up to $7.3M a year (upper bound assuming every store wage rises 4%); unoffset, it would leave FY2028 margin near 2.5%
- Carrier's proposed 3% rate rise, about $0.3M
- The plan meets the target 'with little room to spare'
