**Recommendation:** Decision today: approve the six actions, including three long-lease exits at $7.5M of the $25M limit. Harlow should fix promotions, shrink and delivery and exit weak stores, not cut store hours; promotions, the delivery fee and shrink go first.

**Alternatives considered:**
- Draft plan (CFO and COO): cut scheduled store hours by 8%: Rejected: could net as little as $4.5M of its $14.5M saving once higher shrink is counted; "A cut treats a minor cause of the decline and feeds a major one: move hours to the high-shrink stores instead"; next step: cancel the 8% hour cut.
- Draft plan: freeze e-commerce at current size: Rejected: "Freezing e-commerce at today's size, as the draft plan proposes, keeps this loss"; instead roll out the tested fee and steer orders to click-and-collect.
- Exit only the six stores whose leases end by 2027: Act at lease end, no vote and no exit cost, $4.4M a year; but without the three long-lease exits the margin would be about 2.9%.
- Exit the three stores whose leases run beyond 2027: Board vote required; $7.5M one-time cost, payback of 3.4 years; recommended as needed to reach 3.0%.
- Exit all nine loss-making stores: Chosen: $6.6M a year for $7.5M one-time; needed to reach 3.0%.
- Category team's case for deep promotions: Rejected: its 1.8% vs 0.2% comparable-sales growth in the deepest-promotion stores "counts sales, not profit".

**Assumptions and uncertainties:**

- Other SG&A held at 4.7% of sales and is left out; steps sum to the change within rounding. Fiscal years end in January
- The pack gives shrink by quartile but not each quartile's hours or sales
- Hour cut: shrink responds to hours as it did in FY2023–25, when hours per $1,000 of sales fell 6.0% and shrink rose 0.5 percentage points; all of that rise is put down to staffing, which makes this an upper bound
- Hour cut: the saving is management's estimate of $14.5M a year, close to 8% of FY2025 store labor of $181.9M
- Hour cut: sales stay at the FY2025 level of $1,503M
- Hour cut: lost sales from longer checkout waits and stock-outs are not counted; they would lower the net saving further
- Risk: stores within three miles of a ValuMart had comparable sales of −4.2%, against +1.1% elsewhere; hold their key prices
- Sales that move online from a store are assumed to earn the same contribution either way, before fulfillment
- Delivery fee: the 4-store, 10-week test holds chain-wide: delivered orders −9%, click-and-collect +14%, online sales −2%, fee $6.40
- Delivery fee: costs per order stay at FY2025 rates; the provider's proposed 3% rise would cost about $0.3M a year
- Delivery fee: lost online sales follow the survey: 70% move to Harlow stores and 30% leave Harlow
- The test was small, so the rollout should be staged and tracked
- Store-level figures are not in the pack, so six and three stores are pro-rated from the nine-store totals
- Thin headroom: about $1.5M above 3.0%; a 4% rise on the whole store-labor bill would cost $7.3M
- No wage, rent or delivery-rate inflation and no sales growth assumed
- Not counted: vendor funding back toward 55% and a lower delivery rate
- We read FY2026 as the fiscal year that starts after the February 2027 meeting

**Next steps:**

- Roll out the $7.99 fee and $100 threshold in stages, tracking orders and sales weekly; push back on the 3% rate rise — E-commerce lead and procurement — Q1 FY2026
- Cancel the 8% hour cut; move hours to receiving and produce rotation in the thinnest-staffed stores — COO — Q1 FY2026
- Plan exits of the six stores at lease end; bring the three long-lease exits to a board vote — CFO and real estate — Q2 FY2026
