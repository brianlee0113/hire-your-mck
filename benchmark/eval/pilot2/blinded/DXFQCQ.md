## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Yes, raise prices in 2027 by segment: +10% for mid-market and enterprise, hold SMB until a test reads out, and retain the promo cohort without a price rise

**Executive summary:**
1. Answer: The recommended plan adds about $3.0M of net ARR in 2027, and up to $0.5M more if the SMB test passes. It gives the same ~$2.0M as the CFO's flat +12% in the downside case and less SMB risk. No pricing option reaches the 15% growth target on its own; new customers must supply the last ~$1.9M.
2. 1. Churn is rising because of one cohort, not because customers broadly reject the price. Core SMB churn went from 2.0% to only 2.1% a month. The 2,100 customers from the 2025 promotion churn at ~4.9% a month and make up 56% of SMB churners. Better onboarding explains only ~30% of their excess churn; the rest is a reaction to the discount ending.
3. 2. Mid-market and enterprise can take a price rise; SMB has not shown it can. Mid-market: a +10% test had no significant effect on conversion (23.1% vs 23.6%), churn is 0.8% a month and NRR is 112%. Enterprise: 0.2% churn, NRR 118%. SMB: Inkwell's $9 plan has cut Palisade's win rate from 51% to 38%.
4. 3. The CFO's $10.3M is really ~$4.2M, and the extra $1.2M over our plan depends on SMB. Contracts not renewing in 2027 and the 7% enterprise cap cut it to $7.6M; churn cuts it to $4.2M. Our plan gives $3.0M. If customers are more price-sensitive than we assume, both give ~$2.0M, so the flat 12% buys upside only if Inkwell has not changed SMB behaviour.
5. 4. Raise in January, gate SMB on a test, and plan for new customers to close the 15% gap. ARR reaches ~$97.0M (+12.8%) before new customers; NRR stays ~113%. Mid-market and enterprise rises start at January renewals. An SMB test runs in Q1–Q2, with an H2 rise only if it passes. Churn triggers can pause any step.

[Title] Appendix A1: Net gain = renewing ARR × retention × (price rise − churn caused by the rise), calibrated on the 2023 and 2024 price evidence
- •  Net gain per segment = R × g × [p − c × (1 + p)]
- –  R = ARR renewing in 2027: SMB 100% of $21.0M; mid-market 85% of $41.0M = $34.85M; enterprise 40% of $24.0M = $9.6M
- –  g = share retained over 12 months at the H1 2026 monthly churn rate, (1 − m)^12
- –  p = price rise; enterprise capped portion (60%) limited to 7%
- –  c = extra share of retained ARR lost because of the rise; break-even when c = p/(1+p)
- •  Sensitivity (c per point of price)
- –  SMB 0.45%: 2023 +8% added 0.5pp churn for 6 months and 0.1pp for the next 6 = 3.6% ÷ 8
- –  Mid-market and enterprise 0.21%: 2024 test conversion 23.1% vs 23.6% = 2.1% relative ÷ 10
- –  Promo cohort: SMB × 2.9 (52% ÷ 18% citing price)
- –  Downside: mid-market and enterprise at SMB sensitivity; core SMB ×2; promo ×1.5
- •  Simplifications
- –  All repricing measured as a December 2027 run-rate, regardless of renewal month; this overstates revenue booked within 2027
- –  Effect of the price rise on new-customer conversion not modelled (no SMB data)
