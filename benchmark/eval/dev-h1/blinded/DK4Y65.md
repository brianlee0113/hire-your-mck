## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Do not launch Parcelbird Now in 2027; invest in the software business, which can reach profitability within current cash

**Executive summary:**
1. The market is growing fast, but no operator makes money in it — Sales grew 42% a year in 2022–25, yet both incumbents lose money on every order (p. 4, 5)
2. A Parcelbird store would lose money on every order, and customers don't stay long enough to repay their acquisition cost — A store loses $6.17 per order at the median; only the founders' plan, beyond any site observed, makes money — Our pop-up lost $12.57 per order, and only 22% of its customers still ordered in month 3 (p. 7–14)
3. Even on the founders' own numbers, the launch breaks the investors' cash constraints — It needs $9.5M against $7.2M spendable above the runway floor; with core burn, cash runs out in month 13 (p. 16)
4. The software business is a better use of the same cash — It breaks even in about month 26 at today's 60% growth and month 36 at 40% — It keeps 12 months of runway as long as ARR grows 34% a year or more (p. 18–21)
5. Decline the launch, back software-first in phased steps, and set triggers for any return to quick commerce — The launch case meets none of its six conditions; a 90-day checkpoint gates the second tranche of hires (p. 23–26)
