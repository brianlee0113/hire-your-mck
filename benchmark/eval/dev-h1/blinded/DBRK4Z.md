## Part 1 · Normalised storyline

**Governing thought:** Fix gross margin and delivery economics, not headcount: firm levers reach about 2.6% by FY2028, and a prepared second wave closes the gap to 3.0%

**Executive summary:**
1. Promotions, shrink and home delivery drove the decline, not wages
2. Gross margin and e-commerce fulfillment explain 79% of the fall; store labor only 13%
3. Fully costed, e-commerce loses about $10M a year, not the reported +6.1%; delivery loses $12 an order
4. The draft 8% labor cut and e-commerce freeze would stall margin near 1.1%
5. The cut saves $14.5M on paper, but we expect $4–8M of it back as shrink, before any lost sales
6. Only margin-first reaches 3.0% and keeps delivery; closing delivery is a fallback needing owner consent
7. Seven firm levers worth $30M reach 2.6%; a prepared second wave closes the gap to 3.0%
8. Gross margin $17.0M, labor $5.5M, delivery $3.3M, store exits $4.4M; range $17–38M
9. About half of the $9.9M second wave is needed; one-time cost of $17M stays inside the $25M cap
10. Four no-regret moves in 90 days keep FY2026 about $5M above the covenant; we ask for three decisions
11. Endorse the plan over the 8% cut, approve delivery repricing, prepare the second wave and 3-store vote
12. A mid-FY2027 checkpoint below about 2.4% triggers the full wave; key risks: ValuMart traffic, vendors
